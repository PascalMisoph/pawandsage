**Quick answer:** The most common houseplant orchid, the **moth orchid** (*Phalaenopsis*), is listed by the ASPCA as **non-toxic to cats, dogs and horses**. That is the orchid you usually find in supermarkets and garden centres.

## What the ASPCA plant database says

- **Scientific name:** *Phalaenopsis sp.*
- **Family:** Orchidaceae
- **Other common names:** moth orchid, moon orchid
- **ASPCA verdict:** Non-toxic to cats

The verdict above comes straight from the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center's entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/phalaenopsis-orchid). ASPCA Poison Control is the reference most US veterinarians use for plant questions.

## "Orchid" is a huge plant family

Orchidaceae is one of the largest plant families, and the ASPCA lists orchids individually. Many orchids on its cat list are marked non-toxic, including *Phalaenopsis*, but that does not automatically cover every plant with "orchid" in its common name. If you have something unusual, look up the exact name in the [ASPCA plant database](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/cats-plant-list).

## The potting mix and the extras

Orchids are often sold with things that are not part of the plant: bark potting mix, plant food spikes, fertilizer and decorative moss. A cat that digs in the pot can swallow bark pieces, and fertilizers are a separate concern from the plant itself. Keep plant care products stored away from cats and call your vet if your cat gets into them.

## A good cat-home flowering plant

Because moth orchids are on the non-toxic list, they are one of the safer flowering plants for a cat home, a real alternative to bouquets that might contain true lilies (see [holiday plants and cats](/blog/holiday-plants-toxic-to-cats)).

## When to call the vet anyway

Non-toxic means the plant is not expected to poison your cat, not that it is food. Call your veterinarian if your cat vomits repeatedly, seems lethargic, stops eating, or if you are not sure the plant really was moth orchid (look-alikes and nicknames cause a lot of confusion). If you think your cat ate something toxic, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply).
