A summer cat sitter checklist is not just "feed cat, scoop litter."

Heat changes the job. Water matters more. Rooms can overheat. Small messes get worse. A sitter needs a simple station and clear instructions, not a novel.

## The Water Setup

Leave one main water station and at least one backup bowl in another room. Tell the sitter to check both every visit.

If your cat drinks poorly or more than one animal shares water, a larger station can help, but it still needs human eyes.

## The Cooling Instructions

Write down:

- Which blinds stay closed.
- Which rooms stay open.
- Where the cat usually rests.
- What temperature is concerning.
- What heat stress signs require a call.

If your cat pants, drools, becomes weak, collapses, or seems disoriented, the sitter should treat it as urgent and contact a vet.

## The Cleanup Kit

Make a small station with paper towels, trash bags, litter scoop, spare litter, a brush if tolerated, and pet wipes for small messes.

**What helps:** The [HICC PET Glove Wipes](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BRML7424?tag=pawandsage21-20) are useful in a sitter kit because they are individually wrapped and shaped like gloves. Keep the instructions simple: paws, coat, face, or rear-area cleanup only if the cat tolerates it. No wiping wounds, irritated skin, or unknown substances.

## The Daily Check

Ask the sitter to send:

- A photo of your cat.
- A water update.
- Food eaten or not eaten.
- Litter box output.
- Any unusual hiding, vomiting, panting, or behavior change.

This is the fastest way to catch a problem without micromanaging.

## What Not To Ask

Do not ask a sitter to force nail trims, baths, medication they are not comfortable giving, or play sessions that make your cat frantic in a hot room. The sitter's job is care, observation, and stability.
