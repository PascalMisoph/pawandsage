Summer is peak travel season, which means a lot of cats are about to spend some time home alone. Cats are more independent than dogs, and that leads to a common, risky assumption: that you can top up the food and water, head off for a week, and they will be fine. They will not. There is a real difference between a cat that is *self-sufficient for a day* and a cat that is *safe for a week*, and the gap is exactly where things go wrong. Here is how to set up genuinely safe summer travel.

## How Long Is Too Long?

The honest answer is shorter than most people hope:

- **Up to ~24 hours:** A healthy adult cat is fine alone with fresh food, water, and a clean litter box.
- **2 to 3 days:** Possible, but only with someone checking in at least once a day.
- **A week or more:** Requires daily in-person care, full stop. No amount of automation covers a week of being alone.

Kittens, senior cats, and any cat with a medical condition need daily eyes on them no matter how short the trip.

## Why a Feeder Alone Isn't a Plan

Gadgets solve the easy problems and none of the hard ones. An automatic feeder handles meals; it does not notice that your cat stopped eating because they are unwell. A fountain provides water until it tips over, runs dry, or fails. A litter box stays usable for a day or two, then becomes something a fastidious cat will refuse to use, leading to accidents and stress.

The things that actually go wrong on a trip — illness, injury, a knocked-over water source, a blocked feeder, or pure loneliness in a sensitive cat — all need a human. Automation is a helpful *layer*, not the whole plan.

## The Smart Setup: Automation Plus a Human

The best approach pairs reliable equipment with a daily visitor.

**Automate the food.** A programmable feeder keeps meals on schedule so your cat is not gorging or starving around a sitter's timing. A [PetSafe Healthy Pet Simply Feed](https://amazon.com/dp/B00VIXRB6O?tag=pawandsage21-20) dispenses portioned meals at set times and holds enough for a long weekend, which also keeps routine-driven cats calmer.

**Secure the water.** Put out more than one water source so a single failure is not a crisis. A [fountain](https://amazon.com/dp/B0146QXOB0?tag=pawandsage21-20) keeps water fresh and appealing — important in summer when intake matters more — and a backup bowl or two covers you if it stops. Our [hydration guide](/blog/get-cat-to-drink-more-water) has more on keeping a cat drinking.

**Add eyes on the home.** A [pet camera](https://amazon.com/dp/B07ZWVGFDJ?tag=pawandsage21-20), some with treat-dispensing, lets you check in, confirm your cat is eating and moving normally, and even say hello. It will not replace a sitter, but it turns "I hope they're okay" into "I can see they're okay."

**Arrange a real human.** For anything past two days, book a trusted sitter or ask a reliable friend to visit daily — to scoop litter, refresh water, top up food, and simply lay eyes on your cat. Leave your vet's details and an emergency contact.

## Heat-Proof the House Before You Go

Summer adds a layer most travel checklists miss: the house itself can get dangerously hot while you are away.

- **Guarantee a cool room.** Keep a shaded, well-ventilated room accessible, and never leave your cat shut in a space that bakes in afternoon sun.
- **Set blinds and airflow** so the home does not turn into a greenhouse on a hot day.
- **Multiple water stations**, kept out of any sun.
- **Ask your sitter to watch for heat** — see the [signs of heatstroke](/blog/heatstroke-in-cats) — during a heatwave.

## Keep Their Routine Familiar

Cats bond to territory far more than to places, which is why most are calmer at home than in a boarding facility. Keep things normal: same food, same feeding times, familiar bedding with your scent, and their usual toys out. A little enrichment helps a bored, alone cat — the same ideas from [keeping your cat entertained while you're at work](/blog/how-to-keep-cat-busy-while-at-work) apply to vacation days too.

Set up properly, a week away does not have to mean a stressed or at-risk cat. Automate what you can, enlist a human for the rest, and heat-proof the home — then actually enjoy your trip.
