**Quick answer:** Most kittens learn fast. VCA Animal Hospitals says most cats prefer eliminating in litter and often find the box on their own. Give your kitten a **low-sided box**, **clumping litter**, a **quiet spot** away from food and bed, and **confine them to one room** at first. Never punish accidents.

## What you need

- **A low-sided box.** VCA says kittens need sides low enough to get in and out easily; Cornell says the same for kittens and older cats.
- **Enough room and litter.** VCA says the box should allow for at least 3 inches of digging material and enough room to enter and turn around.
- **Clumping litter.** Most cats prefer clumping-style litter (VCA), unscented (Cornell).
- **One more box than cats**, and **one on each floor** (VCA).

## The first-week plan

1. **Start in one room.** VCA recommends confining a new kitten to a room while they adapt, ideally the room where the box will stay, and not letting them roam unsupervised until they use the box consistently.
2. **Show them the box** when you bring them home by placing them near it (VCA).
3. **Keep it clean.** Scoop at least daily (ASPCA).
4. **Keep it away from food, water and bed** (VCA).
5. **If they miss,** clean with an enzymatic product and adjust the setup. VCA says do not punish your kitten or make them sniff the area.

## Not working?

VCA suggests trying a different litter, removing any cover, or moving the box, or setting up a "buffet" of box styles to see what your kitten prefers. If accidents continue, see your veterinarian to rule out a medical cause.

## Robot litter boxes and kittens

VCA notes not all robotic litter boxes are suitable for young kittens. Check the age and weight limits before using one. More in our [Litter-Robot comparison](/blog/litter-robot-4-vs-5-vs-evo).
