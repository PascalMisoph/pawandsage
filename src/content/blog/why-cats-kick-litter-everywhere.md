**Quick answer:** Digging and covering is normal cat behavior, so a bit of scatter is a good sign. Litter ends up on the floor when the box is **too small**, the **sides are too low** for a vigorous digger, or the litter is **too deep**. Fix the box first, before blaming your cat.

## Kicking is normal; not covering can be a warning

Cornell Feline Health Center lists **refusing to bury waste** among the signs a cat dislikes something about its box. So a cat that digs and covers is using the box the way it should. You want to contain the mess, not stop the digging.

## The four fixes that actually help

1. **A bigger box.** The AAFP/ISFM-based owner guide says boxes should be **1.5 times the length of the cat** from nose to base of tail and that many commercial boxes are too small. In a cramped box, every scratch sends litter over the rim.
2. **Higher sides (if your cat can manage them).** VCA Animal Hospitals notes that a backsplash or high-sided box keeps deposits in for cats that eliminate standing up; the same design keeps flung litter in. Kittens, seniors and cats with mobility problems need low sides (Cornell, VCA). See [litter boxes for cats that pee high](/blog/best-litter-box-for-cats-that-pee-high).
3. **Less litter.** The ASPCA says cats usually prefer **one to two inches** of litter; overfilled boxes spill.
4. **A litter mat at the exit.** Catches what sticks to paws.

## Should I use a covered box to stop scatter?

Covers do cut scatter, but Cornell notes that from the cat's point of view **covers hold odors in**, and the ASPCA lists hoods among common reasons cats avoid boxes. If you try one, watch closely and go back to an open box at the first sign of avoidance. Enclosed [litter box furniture](/blog/best-litter-box-furniture) works for some cats; the same caution applies.

## Is it the litter?

Many cats dislike dusty or scented litter (AAFP/ISFM-based guide), so a heavier, low-dust, unscented litter is worth trying. If you switch, do it gradually (Cornell); see [how to transition a cat to a new litter](/blog/how-to-transition-cat-to-new-litter).
