**Quick answer:** The best cat advent calendar is one with 24 simple, string-free toys you can check before your cat gets them, or a DIY calendar of treats your cat already eats. On Amazon, most 2026 cat advent calendars are toy calendars (bell balls, mice, catnip toys, feather teasers) at gift prices. Order by mid-November so it arrives before December 1.

Cat advent calendars are more for us than for our cats. Your cat doesn't know it's December. But a small daily surprise gives you a reason to play every day of a busy month, and that part is good for your cat. Feline welfare guidelines describe daily play and novelty as part of a healthy indoor life.

This guide covers what's really inside the popular calendars, which ones to consider, the 5-minute safety check every calendar needs, and a DIY version for food-motivated cats.

- **Most "cat advent calendars" are toy calendars:** 24 small toys behind 24 doors.

## How to Choose a Cat Advent Calendar

Four things matter more than the box design:

- **Toys vs treats.** Read the item description, not just the title. Several listings mention "treats" in the title but list only toys in the description.
- **Toy types your cat actually plays with.** A cat who ignores mice won't change in December. If your cat loves batting things across the floor, look for balls and crinkle toys. A stalker who lives for the wand needs teasers you'll use together.
- **As few strings and loose bells as possible.** You'll remove them anyway, so a calendar full of string-on-a-stick toys gives you less to use.
- **Unscented.** Some cats react badly to strong fragrances. Skip calendars or toys that advertise a scent other than catnip or silvervine.

## The 5-Minute Safety Check (Do This on Day One)

Open every door the day the calendar arrives, check each toy, then close the doors again. Your cat won't mind that you peeked. Here's what to look for:

1. **Strings, ribbon, elastic and yarn.** Cut them off or set the toy aside. Cornell's Feline Health Center describes how tinsel and similar string-like material can lodge in the intestines and perforate them. VCA explains that these "linear foreign bodies" often need emergency surgery. Cornell's rule of thumb: "If it's not safe for a child, it's not safe for a cat."
2. **Bells and small parts.** Tug on bells, eyes, pom-poms and glued decorations. Anything that comes off in your hand can come off in your cat's mouth.
3. **Feathers and teaser attachments.** Keep them for interactive play only, and store them away afterwards.
4. **Catnip toys.** Most cats are fine with catnip. Some get overexcited, and some don't react at all. Watch the first session.
5. **Hats, collars and costumes.** If a calendar includes wearables, use them only briefly with a relaxed cat, or skip them. Never leave anything around a cat's neck unattended.
6. **The box itself.** Cardboard doors and plastic trays get chewed. Store the calendar on a high shelf or in a closed cupboard.

**If your cat swallows string, ribbon or tinsel, call your vet right away.** If you see string under the tongue or coming out of the back end, don't pull it. That can make the injury worse.

## DIY Cat Advent Calendar: 24 Days of Treats and Play

A homemade calendar suits food-motivated cats, seniors who don't play much, and cats with sensitive stomachs, because you choose every item. You need 24 small envelopes, paper bags or a muffin tin with a cardboard cover, plus a mix of:

- **Lickable treats,** given from the tube as a bonding moment.
- **Single-ingredient freeze-dried treats,** crumbled into a puzzle feeder.
- **Toys you already own,** rotated back in after a week in a drawer.
- **"Experience" days:** a new cardboard box, a paper bag (handles cut off), a sunny window seat, a 10-minute wand session.

**Keep treat days small.** Treats add calories, so account for them in your cat's daily food, especially for cats who are overweight or on a prescription diet (ask your vet first). Introduce any new treat slowly.

### A sample 24-day plan

| Days | Theme | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 7, 13, 19 | Lickable treat | One tube shared over a few minutes of petting |
| 2, 8, 14, 20 | Foraging | A few freeze-dried pieces in a [treat dispenser toy](/blog/best-treat-dispenser-toys-indoor-cats) |
| 3, 9, 15, 21 | Wand session | 10 minutes of hunt-catch-eat play |
| 4, 10, 16, 22 | New-to-them toy | A toy from the drawer they haven't seen in a week |
| 5, 11, 17, 23 | Box or bag day | A new cardboard box or paper bag, handles removed |
| 6, 12, 18, 24 | Window or warm spot | A blanket on a sunny perch, or a fresh [window perch](/blog/cat-window-perch) setup |

## Toy Calendar vs DIY Calendar: Which Is Better?

| | Store-bought toy calendar | DIY treat & play calendar |
|---|---|---|
| Effort | Low: open a door a day | Medium: an hour to set up |
| Control over contents | Low: check each toy | Full |
| Best for | Playful cats, gift-givers | Food-motivated, senior or sensitive cats |
| Safety work | Check all 24 toys on day one | Choose safe items from the start |
| Leftovers after December | 24 small toys to rotate | Treats and toys you already use |

**Our take:** for a gift, a store-bought toy calendar is fun and easy, as long as the recipient does the safety check. For your own cat, a DIY calendar usually gets used more, because it's built around what your cat already loves.

## Getting the Most Out of It

- **Use the door as a play cue.** Open it at the same time each day, ideally before a meal, and play for 5–10 minutes. That builds a routine and matches the natural hunt-eat-groom-sleep sequence.
- **Don't hand out all the toys for good.** Put yesterday's toy away when you open today's, then bring them back in rotation through January. That's about 24 "new" toys for the price of one box.
- **Multi-cat homes:** buy one calendar per cat, or play with each cat separately so a shy cat gets their turn.
- **Kittens:** skip anything with small parts, and supervise all play. Our [new kitten checklist](/blog/new-kitten-checklist) covers kitten-safe toys.
