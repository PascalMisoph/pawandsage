**Quick answer:** Scoop solids and clumps **at least once a day**. Once a week (ASPCA) or every 1–4 weeks (AAFP/ISFM-based owner guide), **empty the box, wash it with warm water and unscented soap or baking soda, dry it, and refill** with fresh litter. Skip ammonia, vinegar and scented cleaners.

## Daily: scoop

1. Scoop all feces and urine clumps. The ASPCA and AAFP/ISFM-based guide both say at least once a day.
2. Top up litter to keep the depth cats prefer: **one to two inches** (ASPCA).
3. Bag and bin the waste. The CDC recommends daily cleaning because the toxoplasmosis parasite becomes infectious 1 to 5 days after shedding. Wash your hands afterward.

## Weekly (or every few weeks): wash

1. Empty all litter.
2. Wash the box with **warm water and unscented soap, baking soda or no soap** (ASPCA), or **soap and hot water only** (AAFP/ISFM-based guide).
3. Rinse well and dry completely.
4. Refill with fresh litter.

## What not to use

- **Scented cleaners:** the ASPCA warns your cat could develop an aversion to the scent.
- **Ammonia and vinegar:** Cornell says they smell like urine and can be irritating.
- **Strong chemicals:** the AAFP/ISFM-based guide says to avoid them.

## Check the box itself

If a freshly washed box still smells, replace it. While you're at it, check size: boxes should be **1.5 times your cat's length** (AAFP/ISFM-based guide).

## Accidents outside the box

Use an **enzymatic cleaner** made for pet odors (ASPCA). Cornell says odors must be **neutralized, not just deodorized**, or the cat may return to the spot. If accidents keep happening, see [cat peeing outside the litter box](/blog/cat-peeing-outside-litter-box).

## Make it easier

Clumping, unscented litter makes daily scooping faster and is what most cats prefer (Cornell; AAFP/ISFM-based guide). See the [best cat litter guide](/blog/best-cat-litter). Automatic boxes have their own routine: [how to clean a Litter-Robot 4](/blog/how-to-clean-litter-robot-4).
