**Quick answer:** Buy the **[Neakasa M1 Plus](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CSKBWBF6?tag=pawandsage21-20)** if you want an open-top box for a big cat at a much lower price. Buy the **[Litter-Robot 4](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0B7835C46?tag=pawandsage21-20)** if you want a sealed globe, the longest track record and Whisker's 90-day trial, and you can pay the premium.

Both boxes sift clumping litter into a sealed waste drawer so you stop scooping daily. They look different, cost different amounts and ask different things of your cat. Specs below come from Whisker's and Neakasa's own pages, checked on November 9, 2026.

## Litter-Robot 4 vs Neakasa M1 Plus: Open Top vs Globe

This is the biggest difference in daily life.

The **[Litter-Robot 4](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0B7835C46?tag=pawandsage21-20)** is a closed globe. Your cat steps over a 6.9 in step, goes in through a 15.75 in opening and uses the litter inside. The globe rotates to sift waste into the drawer after each visit. A sealed design keeps litter and smell contained, but some cats dislike entering a round, enclosed space.

The **[Neakasa M1 Plus](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CSKBWBF6?tag=pawandsage21-20)** is open on top. Neakasa says the entrance stays open 24/7, so a cat can't get shut in, and it describes the layout as friendlier to Maine Coons and Ragdolls. Cornell's Feline Health Center notes that many cats prefer uncovered boxes because an open box lets odors escape and gives them a 360-degree view.

The trade-off is that an open top gives litter and smell more ways out. Neakasa counters with a built-in sealing strip and an anti-tracking mat. We haven't verified those claims in a home, so read them as the maker's description.

If your cat has refused covered boxes before, start with the open-top option. If you have a curious dog or toddler who would dig in an open box, the globe is easier to keep out of reach.

## Litter-Robot 4 vs Neakasa M1: Safety and Reliability

Both makers describe sensors that pause cleaning when a cat is present. Neakasa lists 6 sets of sweeping infrared sensors plus 4 weight sensors on the M1 Plus and a pinch-free controlled rotation system. Whisker's chart lists waste-level sensing and weight tracking on the Litter-Robot 4.

Reliability is where reviewers differ most. Wirecutter names the Litter-Robot 4 its top pick overall. It also documented incidents with the original Neakasa M1 in which the drum over-rotated, and dropped it as a pick. Neakasa says it updated the firmware and removed teeth from the gear in its newer model.

What that means for you:

- Buy the M1 Plus listing, not an old M1 from a third-party seller.
- Install every firmware update right after setup; Neakasa asks for this itself.
- Leave automatic mode off for the first few days and watch how your cat behaves around it.

## Odor, Litter and Cleaning Rules

**Litter-Robot 4:** Whisker recommends standard-weight clumping clay. It generally advises against plant-based, non-clumping, newspaper and wood-pellet litters. If you want a litter built for the machine, [Purina Tidy Cats Designed for Litter-Robot](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DVZD2LJR?tag=pawandsage21-20) exists for that purpose.

**Neakasa M1 Plus:** Neakasa recommends fast-clumping, granular litter such as bentonite or mineral litter. Its filter sifts particles up to 3 mm in diameter and 10 mm in length. It says the box is not compatible with highly absorbent, non-clumping, loosely clumping, newspaper or wood-pellet litters. Neakasa also asks you to fill to the scale line (about 5 to 6 kg of litter the first time).

Both need clumping litter. Switching litter types later is where owners most often run into trouble, so pick a litter and stick to it.
