**Quick answer:** Buy a **[heated cat bed](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07HMVNXXS?tag=pawandsage21-20)** if you want the bed to add a little warmth in a cool room, especially for a senior, kitten or thin cat. Buy a **[self-warming pad](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PF1KYYK?tag=pawandsage21-20)** if you want a no-cord layer that reflects the cat's own body heat. If you have no power, the heated bed cannot help; the self-warming pad still can, and a **[microwavable disc](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00008AJH9?tag=pawandsage21-20)** is the usual backup.

Both products are sold as "warm beds," and they work in completely different ways. This comparison uses K&H's official explanations, because K&H makes widely sold versions of each and publishes the temperature and design details. **Key takeaways**

- **Self-warming = insulation + a reflective layer.** No plug, no added heat.
- **Heated = a low-watt thermostat.** The K&H Thermo-Kitty uses 4 watts and, K&H says, warms to about 102°F in use.
- **A self-warming pad cannot heat a cold room.** A heated bed can take the edge off one sleeping spot.
- **Cords and burn risk belong only to the heated bed.** The AVMA still cautions about heated pet mats.
- **For outages,** the self-warming pad and a microwavable disc keep working; the plug-in bed does not.

## How a Self-Warming Pad Actually Works

A self-warming bed is closer to a good sleeping bag than to a radiator. There is no heating element. K&H's explanation is straightforward: insulation holds the cat's body heat, and a metalized reflective layer sends that heat back toward the cat. Warmth builds while the cat stays on the pad and fades after the cat leaves.

That is why placement matters more than the product photo. A pad on a cold, drafty floor under a window will lose heat as fast as the cat makes it. The same pad inside a high-sided bed, a cave, or a carrier, off the floor, has a chance to do its job. K&H also notes that some pads make a slight crinkle, which many cats like and a few noise-sensitive cats do not.

What a self-warming pad will not do:

- Heat the room.
- Warm up before the cat arrives.
- Help much if the cat only sits on it for a minute.
- Replace a veterinarian's advice for a cat that is shivering, lethargic or unusually cold to the touch — those can be hypothermia signs (PetMD).

## How a Heated Cat Bed Actually Works

A heated cat bed adds a small amount of electric heat in a controlled range. The **[K&H Thermo-Kitty](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07HMVNXXS?tag=pawandsage21-20)** is a useful example because K&H publishes the numbers: a thermostatically controlled 4-watt heater, a surface that stays about 10 to 15°F above room temperature when empty, and about 102°F — roughly a cat's normal body temperature — when the cat is on it. K&H says the beds are not pressure sensitive; they give off a little heat while plugged in so the cat can find them, and the cat's body then insulates the surface so it can rise to that target.

The 20-inch Thermo-Kitty we link is a cup-style bed with 6-inch foam walls and a removable, washable cover. The heater comes out for washing and for summer use. K&H says the product is safety certified (MET listed). Even so, the AVMA advises caution with heated pet mats because burns are possible. Leave an unheated bed in the same room, route the cord out of reach, and unplug a damaged unit.

Do not use a human heating pad or electric blanket. Those can overheat and are not designed for a cat that may nest, chew or fail to move away.

PetMD's ranges are the context for that table: many healthy adult cats are uncomfortable below about 45°F, and seniors, kittens and sick cats can feel it from 50 to 60°F. If your indoor temperature is already in a comfortable human range, a self-warming pad may be enough. If you keep a spare room cold, or the cat is older and bony, the heated bed is the more logical tool.

## Can You Use Both Together?

Yes, and many winter setups work better that way than as a forced choice. Put the heated cup where the cat already sleeps at night, and drop the self-warming pad in a second favorite spot — a sofa, a carrier, or a condo on a [cat tree](/blog/cat-tree-black-friday-deals). The pad also travels; the plug-in bed usually does not.

Do not stack a heated pad under a self-warming pad unless the maker of both products says that is allowed. Extra layers can change how a thermostat reads the surface. If you want a softer heated bed, use the official cover and a thin familiar blanket on top, then feel the surface. It should stay warm, not hot.

Introduce either product the same way you would a new bed: place it in a quiet, draft-free spot, add a blanket that already smells like the cat, and let them find it. Picking a stiff senior up and setting them on a new heater is a good way to teach them to avoid it.
