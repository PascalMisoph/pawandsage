**Quick answer:** A tiny piece, occasionally, and only if it agrees with your cat. VCA Animal Hospitals lists **cheese** among foods that are safe and often enjoyed by cats. But the ASPCA warns that **dairy can cause diarrhea or other digestive upset**, and cheese is **very calorie-dense**, so treat it as a "sometimes" food.

## Lactose: why cheese can upset some cats

The ASPCA explains that pets do not have significant amounts of **lactase**, the enzyme that breaks down milk sugar, so milk and dairy products can cause diarrhea or other digestive upset. VCA says most cats are lactose intolerant. If your cat has loose stools or vomits after a bite of cheese, skip it.

## Calories add up fast

This is the part most people underestimate. WSAVA's treat guide lists **cheddar cheese at about 120 calories per ounce (28 g)**, against a daily treat allowance of roughly 18 to 28 calories for an average adult cat. A single ounce is several days' worth of treats. A pea-sized piece is plenty, and lower-calorie treats are a better everyday choice.

## Cheeses and cheese foods to skip

- **Anything with garlic, onion or chives** (herbed cream cheese, flavored spreads): the ASPCA says Allium species can damage red blood cells and cats are more susceptible.
- **Salty, processed cheese snacks:** the ASPCA lists salty foods among foods to avoid.
- **Cheese as a pill pocket every day:** ask your vet about lower-calorie options if your cat needs daily medication.

## How much is "a little"?

Human food counts toward your cat's treat budget. VCA Animal Hospitals says treats and snacks should make up no more than **10% of daily calories**, Cornell Feline Health Center gives a rule of thumb of **10 to 15%**, and WSAVA says **under 10%**. WSAVA's own table puts that at roughly **18 to 28 calories a day** for a healthy adult cat weighing 2.5 to 7 kg (about 5.5 to 15 lb). Your veterinarian can tell you what your cat needs.

## When to call the vet

Call your veterinarian if your cat vomits repeatedly, has diarrhea that does not settle, seems lethargic or stops eating, or if the food contained anything toxic such as onion, garlic, chocolate, grapes or xylitol. For a suspected poisoning, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply) or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies).
