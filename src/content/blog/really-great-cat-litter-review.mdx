## Tracking and Dust: The Main Reason People Switch

Tuft & Paw says its uniquely shaped pellets do not stick to paws and that the litter is triple-screened to remove dust. That is the core promise: no dust cloud when you pour, and less grit in your bed or on your socks. We have not measured either. What we can say is that larger pellets generally track less than fine clay granules, and that the brand puts its money where its mouth is with a 30-day money-back guarantee on the first purchase.

If someone in your home is dust-sensitive, the Charcoal formula is the cleanest test: no scent, no clay.

## Odor: Charcoal vs Lavender vs OdorStop

**Charcoal** relies on activated carbon to absorb smell without fragrance. Many cats prefer unscented litter, so this is our default suggestion.

**Lavender** uses dried lavender petals at what Tuft & Paw says is a 1% concentration, not essential oils. The brand addresses lavender-safety questions directly on its FAQ. If your cat is already picky about the box, start unscented anyway.

**[OdorStop](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=59149&awinaffid=3141803&ued=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.tuftandpaw.com%2Fproducts%2Freally-great-cat-litter-odorstop)** is the brand's answer to multi-cat smell. It blends charcoal tofu pellets with pea litter and low-dust bentonite. That bentonite makes it more like a hybrid than a pure tofu litter, so read the bag before flushing anything.

Odor control still depends mostly on scooping daily and having enough boxes. Cornell's Feline Health Center lists an unpleasant box (dirty, wrong litter, wrong spot) as a common reason cats go elsewhere.

## Flushing: Read the Rules Before You Flush

Tuft & Paw says the tofu formulas completely break down in water and are septic-friendly when flushed in small amounts. Its own guidance also says: do not flush clumps larger than a billiard ball, check local rules (it names California and some other municipalities), and be careful with outdoor or raw-fed cats because of toxoplasmosis. When in doubt, bag it. Never flush clay or crystal litter. More detail: [tofu vs clay cat litter](/blog/tofu-vs-clay-cat-litter).

## Automatic Litter Boxes: Mostly a No

Tuft & Paw's FAQ says most self-cleaning boxes are designed for fine, clay-style litter and that its pellets may not work reliably in them. Whisker's guidance for Litter-Robot specifically lists Really Great Cat Litter as not recommended because it will not sift properly. If you own a Litter-Robot, use a clay from [best litter for Litter-Robot](/blog/best-litter-for-litter-robot) instead.

## How to Switch Without a Litter Box Strike

Tuft & Paw recommends mixing small amounts of the new litter into the old over about four weeks, then emptying the box and refilling with 1 to 2 inches of Really Great Cat Litter. For firmer clumps, the brand suggests keeping the litter around 3 to 4 inches deep and letting clumps sit a few minutes before scooping. If your cat starts eating the pellets, stop using it and call your vet; the brand warns that eating a large amount can cause a blockage.

## Who Should Skip It

- **Litter-Robot and most globe or rake automatic boxes.** Use clay.
- **Budget-first buyers.** A bag is pricier than mainstream clay; compare with [pidan on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0B9MVXJNJ?tag=pawandsage21-20) or an unscented clay like [Dr. Elsey's Ultra](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0009X0QSY?tag=pawandsage21-20).
- **Cats that hate pellets.** Some cats raised on fine clay refuse larger textures. Transition slowly or stay with clay.
- **Crystal-litter fans who want color health monitoring.** That is a different product type; see [PrettyLitter vs Tidy Cats](/blog/pretty-litter-vs-tidy-cats).
