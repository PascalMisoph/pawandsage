**Quick answer:** Some cats simply never cover. But if your cat **used to cover and stopped**, Cornell Feline Health Center lists **refusing to bury waste** alongside crying in the box, perching on the edge and going right next to the box as signs your cat may dislike something about the box. Check cleanliness, litter, size and comfort, and see your vet if anything else has changed.

## What to check first

- **Is the box clean enough?** Some cats refuse a box containing any waste (Cornell). Scoop at least daily (ASPCA).
- **Did you change litter recently?** Cornell notes cats may dislike a litter, especially after a sudden brand switch. Most prefer unscented litter; many dislike scented or dusty litter (AAFP/ISFM-based guide).
- **Is there enough litter to dig in?** The ASPCA says cats usually prefer one to two inches; much less can make covering awkward.
- **Is the box big enough?** It should be **1.5 times your cat's length** (AAFP/ISFM-based guide), with room to turn around (VCA).
- **Does your cat get out fast?** If another pet waits outside the box, your cat may skip the covering step. Give the box more than one exit (Cornell).

## Pain and age

Digging uses joints. Cornell's cognitive dysfunction page notes arthritis can make it hard to use the box, and that cats 10 years and older with cognitive decline may have litter box lapses. If not covering comes with stiffness, less jumping, or accidents, book a vet visit.

## Should I cover it for them?

Yes, scoop promptly. Don't try to "teach" covering by moving your cat's paws; it adds stress around the box.
