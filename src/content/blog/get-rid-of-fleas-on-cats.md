Fleas are a summer problem because fleas love what summer offers: warmth and humidity. A single female flea lays up to fifty eggs a day, and most of an infestation — the eggs, larvae, and pupae — is not on your cat at all but tucked into your carpet, bedding, and floor cracks. That is why fleas feel impossible to beat if you only treat the cat. Win the summer flea fight by treating the cat *and* the environment at the same time, and by avoiding one dangerous mistake.

## First, Confirm It's Actually Fleas

Before you treat, make sure fleas are the cause. The giveaway is **flea dirt** — the digested blood fleas leave behind, which looks like fine black pepper in the fur, especially around the tail base and neck.

The reliable home test: comb your cat over a damp white paper towel and collect the specks. If they smear reddish-brown, that is flea dirt, and you have your answer even if you never see a live flea. A fine-toothed [flea comb](https://amazon.com/dp/B0002RJM7I?tag=pawandsage21-20) is the right tool for this, and it pulls live fleas and dirt out of the coat at the same time.

## The Safe Way to Get Rid of Them

Getting rid of fleas is a three-front job. Skip any one of these and they come back.

### 1. Treat the cat with a vet-approved product

This is the heart of it, and the part to get right. **Modern vet-recommended flea preventatives are by far the most effective and safest option** — they break the flea life cycle in a way that combs and sprays cannot. Talk to your vet about the right product for your cat's age and weight, and follow the dosing exactly.

> ⚠️ **The mistake that kills cats:** Never use a flea product made for dogs on a cat. Many contain **permethrin**, which is highly toxic to cats and can be fatal. Don't let your cat groom a dog that's just been treated, either. Only ever use products labeled for cats, and when in doubt, ask your vet first.

Be skeptical of cheap "natural" spot-ons and ultrasonic flea collars — many are ineffective at best, and some essential-oil products are themselves toxic to cats.

### 2. Comb daily to clear the live fleas

While the preventative does its work, a [flea comb](https://amazon.com/dp/B0002RJM7I?tag=pawandsage21-20) gives immediate relief and lets you track progress. Comb over a bowl of soapy water and dunk what you catch — fleas can jump back out of plain water. Daily combing also removes flea dirt that would otherwise irritate the skin. It is the gentlest tool there is, which makes it ideal for kittens too young for chemical treatments, under vet guidance.

### 3. Treat the home, where most of the fleas live

Because the majority of an infestation is in the environment, the cat treatment alone will not finish the job:

- **Wash all bedding** — your cat's and yours — in hot water.
- **Vacuum thoroughly and often**, especially carpet edges, under furniture, and floor cracks. The vibration even coaxes pupae to hatch so the treatment reaches them. Empty the vacuum outside afterward.
- **Stay consistent for several weeks.** Flea pupae can lie dormant and hatch later, so a single clean-up rarely ends it. Keep treating and vacuuming through the full life cycle.

## Why Summer Makes It Worse — and How to Stay Ahead

Heat and humidity speed up the flea life cycle dramatically, so an infestation snowballs faster in July than it ever would in January. The flip side is that prevention is much easier than eradication.

- **Keep year-round prevention going** if your vet recommends it — even indoor cats, since fleas come in on us.
- **Comb weekly** as an early-warning system, so you catch two fleas before they become two hundred.
- **Treat every pet in the home.** Fleas move between animals, so one untreated pet keeps the cycle alive for everyone.

Fleas are also worth catching early because heavy scratching and over-grooming look a lot like other summer skin issues.
