## How to Make a Holiday Drive Easier (ASPCA Tips)

- **Practice:** take a series of short drives first, then lengthen them.
- **Secure the carrier** so it will not slide or shift in a sudden stop. A belted carrier on the back seat or on the floor behind a front seat is the usual setup.
- **Pack a travel kit:** food, bowl, waste bags, any medication, travel documents and something familiar like a favorite pillow or toy.
- **Feed light:** the ASPCA suggests a light meal three to four hours before departure and no feeding in a moving car.
- **Crossing state lines?** Bring rabies records and ask your vet whether a health certificate is needed.
- **Never leave your cat alone in a parked car.** Even with windows open, a car can overheat fast.

Bring a small litter setup for long drives. A disposable tray or a small box with familiar litter works for rest stops; a [tall-walled box](/blog/best-litter-box-for-cats-that-pee-high) is better at your destination if your cat kicks.
