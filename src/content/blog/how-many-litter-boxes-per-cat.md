**Quick answer:** **One litter box per cat, plus one.** Cornell Feline Health Center says this decreases competition and gives each cat a box of its own. VCA Animal Hospitals adds a box **on each floor**, and the AAFP/ISFM-based owner guide says to keep boxes in **different locations**, not lined up side by side.

| Cats | Boxes (n+1) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 3 |
| 3 | 4 |

## Why boxes next to each other count as one

The AAFP/ISFM-based owner guide says cats consider boxes close to each other **one large litter box**. Three boxes in a row in the laundry room is, to your cats, one toilet with one entrance that another cat can block. Spread them out.

## Multi-level homes and older cats

- Put a box **on each level** (VCA; AAFP/ISFM-based guide; the ASPCA says you may need one per level).
- For an older cat, place a box on the level where it spends the most time, because stairs may be hard (AAFP/ISFM-based guide).

## Does one cat really need two boxes?

That is what n+1 means: a second box is a backup when the first is dirty or blocked, and Cornell notes some cats refuse a box with any waste in it.

## Can an automatic litter box count?

Yes, as one of the boxes. The ASPCA says many cats accept self-cleaning boxes readily but advises switching back to a traditional box if your cat starts eliminating outside it. Keep at least one regular box. Compare self-cleaning options in [best automatic litter box for multiple cats](/blog/best-automatic-litter-box-multiple-cats).

## Making extra boxes livable

If boxes have to sit in living areas, [litter box furniture](/blog/best-litter-box-furniture) can hide them. Remove the lid if your cat avoids it, since covers hold odors in from the cat's point of view (Cornell). See also [where to put a litter box](/blog/where-to-put-litter-box).
