**Quick answer:** Yes, a little. VCA Animal Hospitals lists **seedless watermelon** among foods that are safe and often enjoyed by cats, offered without seasonings, and recommends choosing **low-calorie** treats.

## How to serve it

- **Seedless only.** VCA specifically lists *seedless* watermelon. Remove any seeds you find.
- **Flesh only, no rind.** WSAVA advises making sure any treat is an appropriate size and shape to reduce the risk of choking. A cat-sized cube of soft flesh is ideal.
- **Plain.** No salt, sugar or fruit salads with other fruits mixed in.
- **Small portions.** VCA says treats should be no more than 10% of daily calories.

Plenty of cats ignore fruit entirely, and that is fine. Cornell Feline Health Center notes cats are obligate carnivores that rely on nutrients found in animal products, so watermelon is a curiosity, not a nutritional need.

## Fruits that are not okay

VCA lists **grapes and raisins** among foods to avoid, and WSAVA adds **currants and sultanas**. If you are making a fruit bowl, keep it out of your cat's reach.

## Summer hydration

If you're offering watermelon because it's hot, it doesn't replace water. See our [summer dangers guide](/blog/summer-dangers-for-cats) and the [signs of heatstroke in cats](/blog/heatstroke-in-cats).

## How much is "a little"?

Human food counts toward your cat's treat budget. VCA Animal Hospitals says treats and snacks should make up no more than **10% of daily calories**, Cornell Feline Health Center gives a rule of thumb of **10 to 15%**, and WSAVA says **under 10%**. WSAVA's own table puts that at roughly **18 to 28 calories a day** for a healthy adult cat weighing 2.5 to 7 kg (about 5.5 to 15 lb). Your veterinarian can tell you what your cat needs.

## When to call the vet

Call your veterinarian if your cat vomits repeatedly, has diarrhea that does not settle, seems lethargic or stops eating, or if the food contained anything toxic such as onion, garlic, chocolate, grapes or xylitol. For a suspected poisoning, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply) or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies).
