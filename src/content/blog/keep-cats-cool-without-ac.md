Plenty of homes do not have air conditioning, and cats lived through summers long before AC existed. The trick is to work *with* how cats actually cool themselves — through cool surfaces, moving air, and water — rather than trying to chill the whole house. Do that, and you can keep a cat genuinely comfortable through a heatwave with mostly free, low-effort changes. Here is the playbook for an AC-free home.

## Stop the Heat Before It Builds

The cheapest cooling is the heat you never let in. A house without AC lives or dies by how you manage the sun.

- **Close blinds and curtains early**, on the sun-facing side, *before* the room warms. Chasing the heat after noon is a losing game; pre-empting it keeps a room several degrees cooler all day.
- **Open up at night.** When the outside air drops below the inside temperature, open windows (safely — see [cat window and balcony safety](/blog/cat-window-balcony-safety)) to flush the day's heat out, then close everything up again in the morning to trap the cool.
- **Shut off heat sources.** Unused electronics, ovens, and incandescent bulbs all add warmth you do not need.

## Engineer a Cross-Breeze

Airflow is your most powerful free tool. A cat barely sweats, so a fan does less to cool their skin and more to lower the room temperature and move warm air out — but that still makes a real difference.

- **Create a through-draft** by opening windows on opposite sides of the home (secured for cats) so air actually moves rather than sitting still.
- **Use a fan to push hot air out**, not just stir it. Pointing a fan out of a window in the evening vents built-up heat.
- **A small, quiet fan** near your cat's resting spot helps a damp-furred cat shed heat. A [low-noise USB pet fan](https://amazon.com/dp/B07P7JLKNH?tag=pawandsage21-20) does this without the racket that sends cats fleeing. Aim it across the area, not directly at the cat.

## Guarantee a Cool Retreat

This is the one a cat cares about most. Cats cool down by lying on cold surfaces, so the job is to make sure they always have access to one.

- **Open up the cold rooms.** Tiled bathrooms, the kitchen floor, a north-facing room, the bottom of a closet — keep these reachable. Prop the bathroom door so the tub and tiles are available.
- **Add a cooling mat** to their favorite napping spot. A [gel cooling mat](https://amazon.com/dp/B09S3Y2TXN?tag=pawandsage21-20) cools on contact with no electricity, giving your cat a deliberately cold surface in the room they already choose.
- **Try a cool damp towel** over a tile or in a shaded spot for a free, refreshable cool patch.

## Keep Water Cold and Everywhere

Hydration carries a cat through a hot day, and it is harder to stay hydrated in a warm home. Make water unmissable:

- **Several bowls in several rooms**, refreshed often so they do not go warm.
- **Ice cubes in the bowl** to keep it cold and give curious cats a reason to drink.
- **A fountain** keeps water cooler and moving, which encourages reluctant drinkers — more tactics in [how to get your cat to drink more water](/blog/get-cat-to-drink-more-water).

## Cool the Cat Directly, Gently

When the house is just hot, a couple of direct tricks help:

- **Damp-stroke them.** Run a wet hand or cloth over the head, ears, and belly; as it evaporates it draws heat away, mimicking a cat's own grooming. Brush long-haired cats first so the water reaches skin.
- **Offer frozen treats.** A frozen lick mat or a broth ice cube cools from the inside while keeping a cat occupied — see [DIY frozen cat treats and ice games](/blog/frozen-cat-treats-ice-games).

## Watch the Cats Who Feel It First

Without AC, keep a closer eye on the high-risk cats — kittens, seniors, flat-faced breeds, and overweight or long-haired cats — and check on them through the hottest hours. Know the [warning signs of heatstroke](/blog/heatstroke-in-cats) so you can act fast if a hot day tips too far.

No AC is no problem for a cat as long as you give them what they actually use: a cold surface to lie on, air that moves, and cold water within reach.
