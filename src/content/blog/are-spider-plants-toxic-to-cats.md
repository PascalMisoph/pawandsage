**Quick answer:** No. The ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center lists the spider plant (*Chlorophytum comosum*) as **non-toxic to cats and dogs**. It is one of the safest common houseplants to keep in a home with a cat.

## What the ASPCA plant database says

- **Scientific name:** *Chlorophytum comosum*
- **Family:** Asparagaceae
- **Other common names:** ribbon plant, spider ivy, Anthericum
- **ASPCA verdict:** Non-toxic to cats

The verdict above comes straight from the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center's entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/spider-plant). ASPCA Poison Control is the reference most US veterinarians use for plant questions.

## Why cats love chewing spider plants

Spider plants have long, arching, grass-like leaves that sway and dangle, which is exactly the kind of moving target cats like to bat and chew. Non-toxic does not mean "free to eat": a cat that chews a lot of any plant can still bring it back up, and shredded leaves are a sign your cat may want more to do.

If your cat targets the plant:

- Hang it or put it on a shelf your cat cannot reach, and let the runners ("spiderettes") trail out of paw range.
- Offer something better to chase. A short play session with a wand toy before you leave the room often does more than any deterrent; see [how much playtime a cat needs](/blog/how-much-playtime-does-a-cat-need).
- Check what else is nearby. A spider plant on a shelf next to a pothos or a peace lily is only as safe as its neighbour.

## Don't confuse it with these

The common name "ribbon plant" is also used for some dracaenas, and the ASPCA lists **dracaena as toxic to cats**. If a plant label just says "ribbon plant," check the scientific name: *Chlorophytum comosum* is the spider plant; *Dracaena* species (including lucky bamboo) are not safe. See [is lucky bamboo toxic to cats?](/blog/is-lucky-bamboo-toxic-to-cats)

## When to call the vet anyway

Non-toxic means the plant is not expected to poison your cat, not that it is food. Call your veterinarian if your cat vomits repeatedly, seems lethargic, stops eating, or if you are not sure the plant really was spider plant (look-alikes and nicknames cause a lot of confusion). If you think your cat ate something toxic, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply).
