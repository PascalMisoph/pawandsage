**Quick answer:** Yes. The ASPCA lists aloe (*Aloe vera*) as **toxic to cats, dogs and horses**. The toxic principles are **saponins and anthraquinones**, and the listed signs are **vomiting, lethargy and diarrhea**.

## What the ASPCA plant database says

- **Scientific name:** *Aloe vera*
- **Family:** Liliaceae
- **Other common names:** aloe
- **ASPCA verdict:** Toxic to cats
- **Toxic principle:** saponins and anthraquinones
- **Clinical signs listed:** vomiting, lethargy, diarrhea

The verdict above comes straight from the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center's entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/aloe). ASPCA Poison Control is the reference most US veterinarians use for plant questions.

## The gel vs. the plant

The ASPCA entry adds one nuance: "The gel is considered edible." The concern is the rest of the plant, the leaf and the yellowish layer under the skin, which is where cats chewing a potted aloe get their dose. That note is about the plant, not a green light to put aloe products on your cat.

Do not apply human aloe gels, sunburn sprays or after-sun lotions to a cat without asking your veterinarian first. Cats groom off whatever is on their fur, and many products contain other ingredients. (If your cat gets sunburn, see [can cats get sunburn?](/blog/can-cats-get-sunburn))

## Keeping aloe in a cat home

Aloe is a succulent people often keep on sunny windowsills, which happen to be a cat's favourite place. Move it to a sunny spot your cat cannot reach, or swap the windowsill for non-toxic plants from the ASPCA list.

## Symptoms to watch for

According to the ASPCA, signs after a cat chews aloe vera can include:

- Vomiting
- Lethargy
- Diarrhea

How sick a cat gets depends on how much it ate, its size and its overall health, which is why poison control asks for those details.

## What to do if your cat ate aloe vera

1. **Move your cat away from the plant** and remove any plant pieces you can easily reach without being bitten.
2. **Call right away:** your veterinarian, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply), or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies; the helpline says it is available 24/7).
3. **Have the details ready:** the plant's name (photo or label), roughly how much was eaten and when, and your cat's weight and health conditions.
4. **Don't try home remedies** or make your cat vomit unless a veterinarian tells you to.
5. **Bring the plant or a photo** if you are told to go to a clinic.
