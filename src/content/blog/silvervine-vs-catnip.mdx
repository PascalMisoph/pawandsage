**Key takeaways**

- **Silvervine reached more cats than catnip** in the 2017 BMC Veterinary Research study (79% vs 68%).
- **Most catnip non-responders still had a chance:** 22 of 31 responded to silver vine.
- **Tatarian honeysuckle and valerian root** were also tested; they worked for some cats, usually at lower rates than silver vine.
- **Short sessions beat a toy left on the floor all week.** Put the stick or sack away when play ends.
- **This is enrichment, not a behavior fix.** A bored indoor cat still needs play, perches and puzzle meals, which we cover in [indoor cat enrichment ideas](/blog/indoor-cat-enrichment-ideas).

## What the 2017 Study Actually Measured

Researchers offered 100 domestic cats plant materials — including silver vine, catnip, Tatarian honeysuckle and valerian root — and scored the classic response: sniffing, licking, chewing, rubbing and rolling. Silver vine was the strongest overall attractant in that group. Catnip was close behind. Importantly for anyone who has bought "the good catnip" and seen nothing, most cats that ignored catnip still responded to silver vine.

The paper also looked at the chemistry. Silver vine's effect is not the same single-compound story people often tell about catnip (nepetalactone). The study discussed compounds such as actinidine in silver vine. You do not need to memorize the molecules. You only need to know that "my cat is a non-responder" is often catnip-specific, not a life sentence.

The study does not tell you which *brand* of stick is best, how many minutes the high lasts in your living room, or whether a kitten will react. It also does not say these plants treat anxiety, spraying or boredom on their own.

## How to Run a Fair Test at Home

A stale bag and a toy that has been on the sofa for a month is a poor test.

1. Pick a quiet time when your cat is already a little restless, not right after a meal or during a nap.
2. Offer **one** item for 5 to 15 minutes: a stick, a stuffed toy, or a pinch of blend on a mouse. Watch. Some cats lick and chew; others roll and rub; a few just sniff and walk away.
3. Put it away. The [AAFP / FelineVMA well-being principles](https://catvets.com/resource/general-principles-of-feline-well-being-position-statement/) treat play and predatory sequences as part of a healthy indoor life. A disappearing toy keeps the sequence interesting, which is the same idea as in [do cats get bored of their toys](/blog/do-cats-get-bored-of-their-toys).
4. If there is no response, try the other plant on another day, not both dumped on the carpet at once.

Kittens often show little interest. Senior cats may still enjoy a gentle rub with a soft toy even if they do not roll. If your cat starts eating large pieces of stick or plastic, take it away and switch to a supervised stuffed toy.

## What a "Response" Looks Like — and What It Does Not

In the 2017 study, researchers scored the familiar sequence: sniffing, licking, chewing, rubbing the face and rolling. At home you will usually see a mix, not a dramatic cartoon "high." Some cats lick a stick, walk away, and come back. Some roll hard for a few minutes and then groom. A cat that sniffs once and leaves is a non-response for that session, not a moral failure on your part.

The paper used **100 domestic cats**. That is a solid sample for this kind of work, and it is still a single study. It does not measure how long the interest lasts in a living room, and it does not compare brands of sticks. If your cat responds, end the session while it is still fun and offer a short [wand chase](/blog/best-interactive-cat-toys) or a [puzzle meal](/blog/best-puzzle-feeders-cats) afterward. That is the predatory sequence — hunt, pounce, "kill," eat — that feline well-being guidelines treat as part of a healthy indoor day, not a pile of herbs left out until they go stale.

If there is still no interest after a fresh silvervine try and a fresh catnip try, you can look later at Tatarian honeysuckle or valerian root, which the same paper also tested. We are not attaching invented percentages to those arms here. Treat them as backup scents, not as a reason to skip silvervine.

## Safety, Holidays and What These Plants Will Not Fix

Silvervine and catnip are for play. They will not replace a [wand session](/blog/how-to-tire-out-a-cat), a [puzzle feeder](/blog/best-puzzle-feeders-cats) or a vet visit if your cat has suddenly stopped eating or hiding more than usual. If you are stuffing a stocking, skip ribbon, string and tinsel ties. Cornell's holiday-hazard notes and VCA's linear foreign body article are why our stocking guide is firm about that. Pair a herb toy with safe wrapping, not with [tinsel on the tree](/blog/is-tinsel-bad-for-cats).

Store the jar or sticks in a dry, closed container. A little goes further than a pile on the carpet, which just gets vacuumed up and can tempt a cat to eat a large amount of loose plant.
