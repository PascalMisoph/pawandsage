**Key takeaways**

- **Heat the perch, not the glass.** A 6-watt thermostatic sill pad warms the bed; it will not turn a single-pane window into a radiator.
- **K&H's heated Kitty Sill** holds up to 40 lb, needs a sill at least 2 inches deep, and uses hook-and-loop or screws. The cord is 5.5 feet; do not use an extension cord.

## Why Window Seats Get Abandoned in Winter

Cats park on sills because the view is interesting, and because sun through glass can feel wonderful at midday. After sunset, or on a north-facing pane, that same glass pulls heat out of the cat. PetMD's cold-weather guidance notes that seniors, kittens, thin and sick cats can feel chilly below about 50 to 60°F, and many healthy adults are uncomfortable below about 45°F. A cat does not have to live in a freezing house to decide that the couch is cozier than the window.

The goal is to keep the view and lose the chill. Ohio State's Indoor Pet Initiative treats looking out as a real environmental need, so giving up the window for the whole winter is a downgrade. A heated perch is one way to keep that need met. A fleece-lined unheated perch in a sunny window, or a bed slightly back from the glass, can be enough in a warm room. For whole-home winter tactics, see [how to keep cats warm without heat](/blog/keep-cats-warm-without-heat). For plug-in versus reflective pads in general, see [self-warming vs heated cat bed](/blog/self-warming-vs-heated-cat-bed).

## Safety: Cords, Windows, Balconies and Other Pets

- **Route the 5.5-foot cord along the wall or sill**, not across a walkway, and not under a rug that can trap heat. If 5.5 feet will not reach a nearby outlet, move the perch or pick a different window. Do not add an extension cord against the maker's guidance.
- **Leave an unheated bed in the same room** so the cat can get off the warmth. The AVMA cautions that heated pet mats can cause burns.
- **Do not use a human heating pad** on a sill or stuffed into a suction hammock.
- **Keep the window closed and locked** when the cat is unsupervised. Screens are not a barrier. For open-window and balcony risks, see [cat window and balcony safety](/blog/cat-window-balcony-safety).
- **Watch other pets.** A dog that can reach the sill can pull a hook-and-loop perch down. Let the fasteners cure, or use the screws, and test the mount before you trust it with a 15-lb jump.
- **Seniors with limited mobility** need a step up they can actually make. A heated sill 3 feet off the floor with no intermediate perch is a trap, not a gift. Our [heated beds for senior cats](/blog/best-heated-cat-beds-senior-cats) guide covers low, high-sided floor beds if jumping is already hard.

## How to Get a Cat to Use the New Perch

Place it at the window the cat already visits, not at a "better" window they ignore. Add a familiar small blanket (not a thick stack that blocks the thermostat). Sit with a wand toy that ends on the perch, or leave a treat there for a few days. If you add an outdoor feeder, do it on the same pane so the heat and the view are in one place.

If the cat uses the perch at noon and abandons it at night, the heater is doing its job only if the surface feels warm, not hot, after the cat has been on it. K&H's pillow test — 20 minutes under a pillow, warm not hot, no extension cord — is the official way to check a new unit.
