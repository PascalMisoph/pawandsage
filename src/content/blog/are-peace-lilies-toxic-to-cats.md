**Quick answer:** Yes. The ASPCA lists the peace lily (*Spathiphyllum*) as **toxic to cats and dogs**. It contains **insoluble calcium oxalates**, which cause mouth pain, drooling, vomiting and difficulty swallowing.

## What the ASPCA plant database says

- **Scientific name:** *Spathiphyllum*
- **Family:** Araceae
- **Other common names:** Mauna Loa peace lily
- **ASPCA verdict:** Toxic to cats
- **Toxic principle:** insoluble calcium oxalates
- **Clinical signs listed:** oral irritation, intense burning and irritation of the mouth, tongue and lips, excessive drooling, vomiting, difficulty swallowing

The verdict above comes straight from the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center's entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/peace-lily). ASPCA Poison Control is the reference most US veterinarians use for plant questions.

## Peace lily vs. true lilies: an important difference

Despite the name, the peace lily is not a true lily. It belongs to the arum family (Araceae), the same family as [pothos](/blog/is-pothos-toxic-to-cats) and [monstera](/blog/is-monstera-toxic-to-cats), and its problem is calcium oxalate crystals that irritate the mouth.

True lilies (*Lilium*) and daylilies (*Hemerocallis*) are a different emergency. The U.S. FDA warns that even a small bite of a leaf or petal, a few grains of pollen licked off the fur, or vase water can cause **fatal kidney failure in cats**, and the ASPCA lists kidney failure for the Easter lily. The FDA also notes that peace lilies are not in the true lily or daylily families and don't cause kidney failure. If you are not sure which plant your cat chewed, treat it as the more dangerous one and call immediately. Our [holiday plants toxicity chart](/blog/holiday-plants-toxic-to-cats) covers true lilies in bouquets.

## What to do

Peace lilies are often gifts, so they turn up suddenly in homes that were cat-safe the day before. If you keep one, it needs a room your cat cannot enter. Otherwise, a non-toxic plant from the ASPCA list is the easier choice.

## Symptoms to watch for

According to the ASPCA, signs after a cat chews peace lily can include:

- Oral irritation
- Intense burning and irritation of the mouth, tongue and lips
- Excessive drooling
- Vomiting
- Difficulty swallowing

How sick a cat gets depends on how much it ate, its size and its overall health, which is why poison control asks for those details.

## What to do if your cat ate peace lily

1. **Move your cat away from the plant** and remove any plant pieces you can easily reach without being bitten.
2. **Call right away:** your veterinarian, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply), or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies; the helpline says it is available 24/7).
3. **Have the details ready:** the plant's name (photo or label), roughly how much was eaten and when, and your cat's weight and health conditions.
4. **Don't try home remedies** or make your cat vomit unless a veterinarian tells you to.
5. **Bring the plant or a photo** if you are told to go to a clinic.
