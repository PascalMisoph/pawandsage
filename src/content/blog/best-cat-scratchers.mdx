**Key takeaways**

- **Scratching is normal**, not bad behavior: cats scratch to mark territory, stretch and maintain their claws, per the ASPCA and Cornell.
- **Offer variety first.** Cardboard, sisal, carpet and wood; horizontal, vertical and slanted. Then buy more of what your cat picks.
- **Sturdy and tall wins** for vertical posts. A post that wobbles gets ignored.
- **Location matters as much as material.** Put a scratcher next to the couch your cat is attacking.
- **Behavior problem?** Read [how to stop a cat scratching furniture](/blog/how-to-stop-cat-scratching-furniture) alongside this list.

## How to Choose a Cat Scratcher

1. **Watch where and how your cat scratches.** Standing up on the couch arm means a tall post; raking the rug means a flat pad.
2. **Match the texture.** The ASPCA notes some cats prefer a vertical grain for raking and others a horizontal grain for picking.
3. **Stability first.** A post that moves once can be ignored forever.
4. **Placement.** Next to the target furniture, near sleeping spots and in the main room.
5. **Make it attractive.** Rub catnip or silver vine on it, play with a wand toy around it, and reward use.
6. **Trim nails.** Regular trims reduce damage while you retrain: [how to trim cat nails at home](/blog/how-to-trim-cat-nails-at-home).
