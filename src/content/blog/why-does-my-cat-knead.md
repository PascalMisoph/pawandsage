**Quick answer:** Kneading ("making biscuits") is **normal**. VCA Animal Hospitals and AAHA explain that **kittens knead their mother while nursing** to stimulate milk flow, and adult cats keep doing it to **recreate that feel-good state**, to **show affection**, and to **scent-mark** with glands in their paws. AAHA says it's a behavior you should let your cat express.

## The reasons, in short

- **Nursing origin.** Kittens knead to stimulate milk production (VCA; AAHA).
- **Feel-good hormones.** VCA says kneading is thought to stimulate **oxytocin**, linked to maternal bonding, and experts theorize adults knead to recreate the hormone release of nursing (AAHA).
- **Affection.** VCA lists showing affection to human and animal family members.
- **Scent marking.** Cats have scent glands around their paws and claws (VCA); kneading favorite bedding, places or people leaves their scent (AAHA).
- **Nesting.** VCA notes cats' ancestors didn't have cozy beds and blankets; kneading may echo preparing a resting spot.

## When kneading means something else

Both sources mention **self-soothing**: VCA says cats that aren't feeling well may purr and knead, and AAHA says stressed cats or cats in pain may knead to comfort themselves. If kneading suddenly increases along with hiding, appetite changes or other new behavior, check in with your vet.

## Kneading with claws: what to do

- **Trim claws regularly.** VCA and AAHA both recommend it; see [how to trim cat nails at home](/blog/how-to-trim-cat-nails-at-home) and [nail clippers vs grinder](/blog/cat-nail-clippers-vs-grinder).
- **Give a designated kneading blanket.** AAHA suggests a thick, plush blanket as your cat's kneading surface; VCA suggests redirecting to a designated area with treats, toys and pheromone sprays.
- **Don't punish.** It's normal behavior.
