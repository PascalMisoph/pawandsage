**Quick answer:** Watch closely for a minute. If your cat is **straining and producing little or no urine**, especially a male cat, this is an **emergency**: VCA Animal Hospitals calls a urethral obstruction "an absolute emergency." Go to a vet now. If your cat is simply resting in the box and urinating normally, look at stress, other pets and age.

> **When it is an emergency:** a cat that keeps straining in the litter box and passes little or no urine needs a veterinarian **right now**. VCA Animal Hospitals calls a urethral obstruction "an absolute emergency." Cornell Feline Health Center lists male cats among those at increased risk of lower urinary tract disease.

## Is it straining or sitting?

- **Straining:** squatting repeatedly, little or no urine (check the litter for clumps), crying, licking the genital area. The ASPCA says cats with cystitis try to urinate frequently and may look as if they are straining with little success, and may have blood in the urine. VCA says repeated straining with little or no urine needs immediate care.
- **Frequent short visits:** the ASPCA notes a cat that keeps entering the box and produces small amounts of urine may have a urinary tract infection, and that kidney stones or a blockage can make a cat visit the box frequently and cry when trying to eliminate.
- **Resting or lying in a clean box:** not something the cited sources list as a diagnosis on its own. Vets look at what else is going on.

## Possible reasons for resting in the box

- **Stress or a new home.** A box can be a small, enclosed space in an unfamiliar room. Give a covered bed or hiding box nearby as an alternative.
- **Another pet.** Cornell asks whether another animal ambushes your cat near the box. A cat guarding or hiding may sit in it. Add boxes in different locations, and make sure there is more than one way out.
- **Age.** Cornell's cognitive dysfunction page lists spatial disorientation and litter box lapses among signs in cats 10 years and older.

If resting in the box is new and you can't explain it, call your vet. It is easier to rule out a medical cause early.

## Make the box comfortable for its real job

Clean daily, keep one to two inches of unscented litter (ASPCA), and use a box big enough for your cat (1.5 times its length per the AAFP/ISFM-based owner guide). See our [best cat litter guide](/blog/best-cat-litter) and [cat peeing outside the litter box](/blog/cat-peeing-outside-litter-box).
