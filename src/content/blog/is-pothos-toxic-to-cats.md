**Quick answer:** Yes. The ASPCA lists pothos (golden pothos, devil's ivy, *Epipremnum aureum*) as **toxic to cats and dogs**. It contains **insoluble calcium oxalates**, which cause intense mouth irritation, drooling and vomiting when chewed.

## What the ASPCA plant database says

- **Scientific name:** *Epipremnum aureum*
- **Family:** Araceae
- **Other common names:** golden pothos, devil's ivy, taro vine, ivy arum
- **ASPCA verdict:** Toxic to cats
- **Toxic principle:** insoluble calcium oxalates
- **Clinical signs listed:** oral irritation, intense burning and irritation of the mouth, tongue and lips, excessive drooling, vomiting, difficulty swallowing

The verdict above comes straight from the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center's entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/golden-pothos). ASPCA Poison Control is the reference most US veterinarians use for plant questions.

## Why pothos hurts

Insoluble calcium oxalates are microscopic, needle-shaped crystals in the leaves and stems. When a cat bites into the plant, the crystals are released into the mouth, which is why the ASPCA's list of signs centres on the mouth: burning, irritation of the tongue and lips, drooling and trouble swallowing, plus vomiting.

The same toxic principle shows up in other popular trailing and tropical houseplants on the ASPCA list, including [peace lily](/blog/are-peace-lilies-toxic-to-cats), [monstera and philodendrons](/blog/is-monstera-toxic-to-cats).

## Living with pothos and a cat

Pothos is hard to make fully cat-proof because it trails. Hanging baskets put dangling vines at exactly the height a cat can reach from furniture. If you keep it:

- Put it somewhere your cat genuinely cannot get to (a closed room or an enclosed cabinet), not just "up high."
- Pick up fallen leaves.
- Consider swapping it for a non-toxic trailing look. On the ASPCA list, the [spider plant](/blog/are-spider-plants-toxic-to-cats) gives a similar cascading effect.

## Symptoms to watch for

According to the ASPCA, signs after a cat chews pothos can include:

- Oral irritation
- Intense burning and irritation of the mouth, tongue and lips
- Excessive drooling
- Vomiting
- Difficulty swallowing

How sick a cat gets depends on how much it ate, its size and its overall health, which is why poison control asks for those details.

## What to do if your cat ate pothos

1. **Move your cat away from the plant** and remove any plant pieces you can easily reach without being bitten.
2. **Call right away:** your veterinarian, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply), or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies; the helpline says it is available 24/7).
3. **Have the details ready:** the plant's name (photo or label), roughly how much was eaten and when, and your cat's weight and health conditions.
4. **Don't try home remedies** or make your cat vomit unless a veterinarian tells you to.
5. **Bring the plant or a photo** if you are told to go to a clinic.
