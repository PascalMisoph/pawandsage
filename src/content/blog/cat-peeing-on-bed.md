**Quick answer:** Treat it as house soiling, not spite. Start with a **vet check** for urinary problems, then fix whatever your cat dislikes about the **litter box**. Beds are soft, absorbent and smell strongly of you, and the ASPCA notes some cats develop a **surface preference** for bedding. Clean the bedding thoroughly and make the box the better option.

> **When it is an emergency:** a cat that keeps straining in the litter box and passes little or no urine needs a veterinarian **right now**. VCA Animal Hospitals calls a urethral obstruction "an absolute emergency." Cornell Feline Health Center lists male cats among those at increased risk of lower urinary tract disease.

## Medical causes first

Cornell Feline Health Center says urinary tract inflammation makes urinating painful and more frequent, and that kidney disease, thyroid disease and diabetes can increase urine volume. The AAFP/ISFM-based owner guide lists feline idiopathic cystitis (FIC), aggravated by stress, as a frequent cause. A cat with a painful bladder may avoid the box and pick a soft spot instead.

## Why the bed?

- **Soft surface preference.** The ASPCA lists bedding, carpet and potting soil among surfaces some cats come to prefer. Cornell suggests that if a cat likes soft surfaces, a piece of carpet remnant in the box can help, then gradually add litter.
- **Something wrong with the box.** Dirty, too small, scented litter, a lid or liner, or a location where another pet ambushes them (Cornell; ASPCA; AAFP/ISFM-based guide).
- **Stress.** Cornell Feline Health Center lists stressful factors such as living in a multi-cat household and changes in routine, like a move, as risk factors for urinary tract disease, and notes some cats soil in response to the smell of new furniture and carpet.

## What to do today

1. **Wash all bedding** and use an **enzymatic cleaner** on the mattress (ASPCA). Avoid ammonia or vinegar, which Cornell says smell like urine.
2. **Block access** to the bed for now (close the door) or cover it with a plastic sheet; Cornell lists sheets of plastic among deterrents.
3. **Fix the box:** scoop daily, unscented litter, a box 1.5 times your cat's length, more than one box (see [cat peeing outside the litter box](/blog/cat-peeing-outside-litter-box)).
4. **Never punish.** Cornell says cats don't soil out of revenge, and punishment adds stress.

## Is it spraying?

The ASPCA describes spraying as backing up to a **vertical** surface with the tail upright. Urine puddles on a bed are usually not spraying. If your cat sprays, Cornell notes the synthetic pheromone Feliway can discourage marking on treated areas; compare products in [Feliway vs Comfort Zone](/blog/feliway-vs-comfort-zone).
