**Quick answer:** Yes. "Lucky bamboo" is not a real bamboo: it is a **dracaena**, and Pet Poison Helpline lists Lucky Bamboo among dracaena names. The ASPCA lists dracaena as **toxic to cats and dogs** because of **saponins**.

## What the ASPCA plant database says

- **Scientific name:** *Dracaena spp. (lucky bamboo is a dracaena)*
- **Family:** Agavaceae
- **Other common names:** corn plant, cornstalk plant, dragon tree, ribbon plant
- **ASPCA verdict:** Toxic to cats
- **Toxic principle:** saponins
- **Clinical signs listed:** vomiting (occasionally with blood), depression, anorexia (not eating), hypersalivation (drooling), dilated pupils (cats)

The verdict above comes straight from the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center's entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/dracaena). ASPCA Poison Control is the reference most US veterinarians use for plant questions.

## Signs that are specific to cats

The ASPCA lists vomiting (occasionally with blood), depression, anorexia, hypersalivation and **dilated pupils in cats**. Pet Poison Helpline adds that a **wobbly gait** has been reported specifically in cats. Dilated pupils or unsteadiness after chewing a plant are reasons to call right away.

## Real bamboo is a different story

The ASPCA lists true bamboo (golden bamboo, *Phyllostachys aurea*) as **non-toxic** to cats. The problem is the name: the stalks in water sold as "lucky bamboo" are dracaena. Meanwhile "heavenly bamboo" and "sacred bamboo" (*Nandina*) appear on the ASPCA **toxic** list. Go by the scientific name, not the label.

## Other dracaenas you may own

Corn plant, dragon tree, and many striped "dracaena" varieties are on the ASPCA toxic list. Snake plant, now often sold as *Dracaena trifasciata*, is also toxic to cats ([is snake plant toxic to cats?](/blog/is-snake-plant-toxic-to-cats)).

## Symptoms to watch for

According to the ASPCA, signs after a cat chews lucky bamboo can include:

- Vomiting (occasionally with blood)
- Depression
- Anorexia (not eating)
- Hypersalivation (drooling)
- Dilated pupils (cats)

How sick a cat gets depends on how much it ate, its size and its overall health, which is why poison control asks for those details.

## What to do if your cat ate lucky bamboo

1. **Move your cat away from the plant** and remove any plant pieces you can easily reach without being bitten.
2. **Call right away:** your veterinarian, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply), or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies; the helpline says it is available 24/7).
3. **Have the details ready:** the plant's name (photo or label), roughly how much was eaten and when, and your cat's weight and health conditions.
4. **Don't try home remedies** or make your cat vomit unless a veterinarian tells you to.
5. **Bring the plant or a photo** if you are told to go to a clinic.
