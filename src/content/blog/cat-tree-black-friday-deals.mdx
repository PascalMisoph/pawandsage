## What Makes a Cat Tree Worth Buying on Black Friday

Indoor cats need vertical space. Ohio State's Indoor Pet Initiative and the FelineVMA's well-being statement both treat climbing, perching and scratching as core environmental needs, not extras. A tree is simply the easiest way to put those needs in one footprint: a high lookout, a hiding cubby, and posts the cat can scratch instead of the couch. Our [cat wall shelves vs cat trees](/blog/cat-wall-shelves-vs-cat-trees) guide is the longer version of that choice.

The Black Friday question is narrower: which tree is still a good tree when the price drops?

**Stability first.** Press the top perch after you assemble it. If the base lifts or the whole unit twists, it is not a deal. A wide, dense base and a corner placement beat a skinny, very tall tree in a walkway.

**Height that fits the room.** The cat needs a high perch and a post long enough to stretch, but the tree also has to clear ceiling fans, hanging lights and window blinds. Mid-height trees around 50 to 60 inches fit many apartments. Taller towers only pay off if the ceiling is high and the base is serious.

**Sisal-wrapped posts.** Cats scratch to condition their claws and to mark territory. Tight sisal lasts longer than loose rope or plush-covered tubes. If furniture scratching is already a problem, read [how to stop a cat scratching furniture](/blog/how-to-stop-cat-scratching-furniture) and put the tree where the cat already scratches, not in a neglected corner.

**A perch, a hide, and a clear way down.** One enclosed cubby plus two open platforms is enough for most single cats. Multi-cat homes need more than one route, so a cat on the top perch is not blocking everyone else.

**A stated weight rating.** If the listing does not say how much weight the top perch can hold, be cautious, especially for a large or heavy cat.

## How to Tell a Real Discount From a Fake One

We will not invent a "usual price" or a target dollar amount. Retailers change list prices often, and Amazon's number from this location is not a U.S. shelf price you should trust in an article.

What you can do:

1. Pick one or two specific trees now and watch them through Cyber Monday (November 30).
2. Compare the sale price with the regular price on the same listing, and with a recent screenshot or a price tracker you already use, not with a vague "was $199."
3. Check shipping and assembly. A bulky tree with a high delivery fee or missing hardware is not cheaper.
4. Read the current return window. A tree that has been opened is hard to send back; measure twice.
5. Look at our [Black Friday cat deals](/blog/black-friday-cat-deals) hub the same week, in case the same tree is listed there with other holiday gear.

If two trees are the same price, buy the sturdier base and better sisal, not the one with more decorative pom-poms.

## Where to Put the Tree So the Deal Gets Used

A discounted tree in the wrong room is still a coat rack. Place it:

- In a room where people already sit, so the cat can perch and watch you.
- Near a window if you can do it without blocking an exit or a heater.
- Next to the furniture that is currently being scratched, then reward the cat for using the sisal.
- Out of a tight walkway, so people do not bump it and teach the cat that the tree is unstable.

Give the cat a reason to go up: a short wand-toy session that ends on the perch, or a familiar blanket on the condo floor. Do not pick the cat up and drop them on the top platform.

If you are buying the tree as a gift, include a note about placement and a window of time to assemble it. For more gift context, see [gifts for new cat owners](/blog/gifts-for-new-cat-owners) and [best gifts for cat lovers](/blog/best-gifts-for-cat-lovers).


## Multi-Cat Homes, Kittens and Large Cats

**Two or more cats** share a tree more peacefully when there is more than one way up and more than one good perch. A single top platform becomes a resource to guard. If your Black Friday budget can stretch, a mid-height tree plus a second scratching post in another room is often calmer than one extra-tall tower.

**Kittens** will climb anything, including the back of the tree and the curtains beside it. Anchor the tree, skip loose hanging toys with long cords they can swallow, and re-check screws after a week of hard use. Cornell and the FelineVMA both treat safe climbing as part of ordinary care, not a special purchase.

**Large or heavy cats** need the weight rating and the base more than they need extra height. A sturdy 55-inch tree with a wide base is safer than a thin 72-inch tower that flexes at the top. If the listing is vague about capacity, keep looking.

## Assembly, Placement Safety and the First Week

Most mid-height trees arrive in a tall box with a lot of screws. Budget an hour, not ten minutes, and put the base on a level floor. After assembly:

- Push the top perch firmly and lean on the posts. Tighten anything that shifts.
- Keep the tree away from unstable shelves, breakable objects and, in December, the Christmas tree. If you are also decorating, our [cat-proof Christmas tree](/blog/cat-proof-christmas-tree) guide is the better place for ornaments, tinsel and tree guards.
- Recheck the screws after a few days. New trees loosen as the cat jumps.
- If the tree has a dangling toy, supervise at first and remove it if the cord is long enough to wrap or swallow.

A Black Friday tree is a long-term piece of furniture. If it wobbles on day one, return it while you still can, and put the budget toward a shorter, heavier tree or a well-mounted shelf.

## When to Skip the Tree and Buy Something Else

Skip a tree, even on sale, if:

- The only open floor is a hallway the tree will block.
- You cannot assemble a large item or get it through the door.
- Your cat is older or arthritic and already prefers low, heated beds — start with a [heated bed](/blog/best-heated-cat-beds-senior-cats) or a low perch, and add height later.
- You were about to buy a second tree when wall shelves would actually connect the cat to a window.

A good Black Friday purchase is the one that is still being used in February. Measure the room, pick a stable mid-height tree or a well-mounted shelf, and treat the discount as a bonus, not the reason to buy a tippy tower.
