**Quick answer:** Most of the time it's harmless. VCA Animal Hospitals says cats have heightened senses and may be **seeing, hearing or feeling something you can't**, such as an insect, a sound in the wall, or ultraviolet light. But in cats **10 years or older**, Cornell Feline Health Center lists **long periods of staring blankly into space or at walls** among the signs of **cognitive dysfunction**, so a new habit in an older cat is worth a vet visit.

## The harmless reasons

- **Hearing.** VCA says cats can hear from about **80 Hz up to 85,000 Hz**, far beyond the human range of about 20 to 20,000 Hz. A mouse, insect or pipe noise behind the wall is easy for them to track.
- **Vision.** VCA notes cats can see ultraviolet light and fine detail, like a tiny spider or ant you would miss.
- **Hunting.** VCA describes cats watching "prey" before getting into the pounce pose; staring is the first step.
- **No reason at all.** VCA says your cat may just be acting mysterious.

## When staring is a sign to call the vet

Cornell says the behavioral signs of cognitive dysfunction tend to become noticeable in cats **10 years or older**. Signs include:

- Long periods of staring blankly into space or at walls
- Spatial disorientation; less interest in play; sleeping more or at different times
- Indifference to food and water
- Urinating or defecating outside the litter box
- Loud vocalizing, often in the middle of the night

Cornell also notes that other conditions, including hyperthyroidism and hypertension, can cause night vocalizing and confusion, which is why a vet exam comes first. If staring comes with twitching skin, sudden frantic running or attacking the tail, mention it too: VCA lists these among behaviors seen with feline hyperesthesia and compulsive disorders.

## What you can do at home

Give that hunting focus somewhere to go. Short play sessions that mimic stalk, chase and pounce satisfy the same instincts; see [how much playtime a cat needs](/blog/how-much-playtime-does-a-cat-need) and our [best interactive cat toys](/blog/best-interactive-cat-toys). For senior cats, Cornell suggests keeping litter boxes easy to reach with low sides.
