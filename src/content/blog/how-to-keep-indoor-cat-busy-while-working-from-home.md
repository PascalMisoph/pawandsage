Working from home creates a strange problem for indoor cats: you are there, but not available.

Your cat sees the person, hears the voice, watches the hands move, and then gets ignored for hours. That is why the keyboard becomes a stage. It works.

The fix is not pretending your cat does not exist. It is giving them a better job before your work block begins.

## Before the First Meeting: Finish the Hunt

Do five to ten minutes of wand play before your first deep-work session. Let your cat catch the toy, then feed breakfast through a puzzle or small hunt.

That sequence matters: hunt, catch, eat, groom, sleep. It gives your cat a completed loop instead of leaving them under-stimulated beside your desk.

## Build a Nearby Yes Spot

Many cats interrupt because they want proximity, not chaos. Put a bed, shelf, box, or perch near the office zone so your cat can be close without being on the keyboard.

If floor space is tight, go vertical.

## Use Food Work for Calls

Keep one quiet puzzle for meetings. It should be easy enough that your cat does not get frustrated and quiet enough that it does not become the soundtrack of the call.

For more active cats, use a treat dispenser before the meeting rather than during it.

## Schedule Cat Breaks Before Cat Breakdowns

Two minutes can prevent twenty. Between calls, offer a tiny routine: toss three treats to find, refresh water, open a curtain, or do one minute of wand play.

Predictable breaks teach your cat that attention comes in reliable windows, not only when they step on the keyboard.

## What Not To Do

Do not reward the loudest behavior with the best play session of the day. If your cat screams, swats, or climbs the monitor and then gets a huge reaction, the pattern strengthens.

Redirect calmly, then improve the setup before the next work block.
