## How to Choose Litter Box Furniture

1. **Measure inside, then measure your box.** Leave at least a few inches around the box so it slides out for washing.
2. **Measure your cat.** Large cats need head room and turning room; an enclosure that is cramped will be avoided.
3. **Check access for you.** A full-width door or lift-off top makes daily scooping realistic.
4. **Ventilation and odor.** Closed furniture keeps smell in. Daily scooping is not optional.
5. **Entry style.** Side holes are simple; corridors catch litter and keep dogs out but are harder for seniors.
6. **Material.** Look for water-resistant surfaces; urine on raw particleboard swells it.

## When a Cabinet Is the Wrong Answer

- **Your cat pees high or over the edge.** Many cabinets help, but a purpose-built high-sided box is the real fix: see [best litter box for cats that pee high](/blog/best-litter-box-for-cats-that-pee-high).
- **The smell is the problem.** Start with litter and routine: [best cat litter for odor control](/blog/best-cat-litter).
- **You want to scoop less.** A self-cleaning box usually will not fit in furniture; compare [Litter-Robot alternatives](/blog/litter-robot-alternatives) and [automatic boxes for multiple cats](/blog/best-automatic-litter-box-multiple-cats).
- **Your cat stops using the box after you add the cabinet.** Remove the doors or go back to the open box; Cornell lists box aversion among the main causes of house soiling.
