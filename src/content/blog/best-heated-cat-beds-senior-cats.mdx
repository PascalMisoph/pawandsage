## Why Senior and Arthritic Cats Feel the Cold Sooner

Cats run warmer than we do. PetMD puts normal feline body temperature around 100.4 to 102.5°F, which is why a sunbeam or a laptop feels like a prize. As cats age, they often lose muscle and fat, move less, and may have arthritis or a chronic illness. PetMD's cold-weather guidance says seniors, kittens, thin-coated and sick cats can be uncomfortable below about 50 to 60°F, while many healthy adults only start to feel it below about 45°F. The AVMA adds that pets with diabetes, heart disease, kidney disease or hormonal disorders may have a harder time regulating temperature.

A heated bed does not treat arthritis. It can make a stiff cat more willing to rest in a well-padded, slightly warm cup instead of a cold floor. If your cat suddenly hides, seems painful, or stops jumping, that is a veterinary issue, not a bedding issue. ## What "Safe Heat" Means in a Cat Bed

The features that matter are boring, and they are the ones to shop for on Black Friday as well as in January:

- **Low wattage.** K&H's standard Thermo-Kitty uses a 4-watt heater. That is a tiny draw compared with a space heater, and it is part of how the bed stays in a narrow temperature band.
- **A thermostat, not a pressure switch.** K&H says its beds are not pressure sensitive. They give off a little heat while plugged in so the cat can find them, then warm to the cat's body temperature when the cat's body insulates the surface. Empty, the Thermo-Kitty is described as about 10 to 15°F above room temperature; in use, about 102°F.
- **A covered, chew-resistant cord and a certified product.** K&H says it safety-certifies the entire product (MET listed, tested to UL/CSA standards), not just a component. That is a better starting point than an unlisted mat with a bare cord.
- **A removable, washable cover** and a removable heater, so you can wash the bed and use it unheated in summer.
- **An escape route.** The cat must be able to leave the warmth. High sides are cozy; a lid that traps a cat next to a heater is not.

The AVMA's cold-weather page still advises caution with heated pet mats because they can cause burns. That warning and K&H's design can both be true: the safer products exist, and you still do not leave a damaged cord, a human heating pad, or a bed with no unheated alternative.

K&H also says you can test a Thermo-Kitty by placing a large pillow on it for about 20 minutes; it should feel warm, not hot, and you should not use an extension cord for that test. Follow the latest official instructions that come with the bed.

## How to Use a Heated Bed Without Creating a New Problem

1. **Leave a cooler bed in the same room.** A cat that gets too warm must be able to move.
2. **Get it off a cold floor** if you can, and out of a draft from a door or window.
3. **Route the cord tightly** along the wall, out of a chewing kitten's reach and out of a walkway. Never run it under a carpet that can trap heat.
4. **Do not use a human heating pad or electric blanket.** Those are built for a person who can remove them.
5. **Inspect the cover, plug and cord.** Unplug at the first sign of damage.
6. **Introduce it gradually.** Some cats ignore a new bed for a week. A familiar blanket on top helps more than picking the cat up and placing them in it.

If your cat has reduced sensation, is recovering from surgery, or cannot move away easily, ask your veterinarian before using any heated product.
