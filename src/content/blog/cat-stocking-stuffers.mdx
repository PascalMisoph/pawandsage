## What Makes a Good Cat Stocking Stuffer?

A good stocking stuffer for a cat is small, cheap to replace and safe if your cat chews it. Cats don't care about wrapping paper. They care about smell, texture and motion. That's why the best picks target one of those three.

Use this quick filter before you buy:

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| No loose string, ribbon or elastic | String-like items can cause an intestinal blockage if swallowed |
| No small detachable parts (bells, eyes, beads) | Cats chew and swallow small pieces |
| Made for cats | Human candy, chocolate and raisins are never stocking filler |
| Fits one purpose | Sniff, kick, chase or snack, so you can tell what your cat likes |
| Easy to supervise | Open the stocking when you can watch the first play session |

Cornell's Feline Health Center lists tinsel, ribbon and string among the top holiday hazards for cats. VCA explains why: a string caught in the gut can bunch the intestine and need emergency surgery. So keep stockings simple and ribbon-free.

## How to Fill a Cat Stocking Safely

1. **Pick one item per sense.** One smell toy, one chase toy, one snack. Three to five small gifts are plenty.
2. **Check every item for loose parts.** Remove tags, ribbons, plastic ties and bells.
3. **Skip decorative filler.** No tinsel, no ribbon, no tissue paper strips. Cornell lists these among holiday hazards.
4. **Hang the stocking out of reach** until you can supervise.
5. **Hold something back.** Save a toy or two for January, when play slows down. Our [do cats get bored of their toys](/blog/do-cats-get-bored-of-their-toys) guide explains toy rotation.

The FelineVMA's feline well-being statement treats a stimulating environment as a basic need, so new toys help most when they're part of a regular play routine.
