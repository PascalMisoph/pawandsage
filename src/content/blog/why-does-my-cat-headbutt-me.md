**Quick answer:** Headbutting, also called **bunting**, is scent-sharing. VCA Animal Hospitals explains that cats have **glands on their cheeks, forehead and chin** that contain pheromones; when your cat headbutts you, they rub those pheromones on you and **accept you into their inner circle**. VCA calls it **a sign of trust**.

## What bunting does

- **Creates a shared colony scent.** VCA says cats bunt other cats in their household to create a common scent, then rub it on you so you smell like the group too.
- **Marks territory.** VCA notes cats rub glands on their cheeks, around the mouth, under the chin and by the ears against prominent items like a couch, bed or desk.
- **Self-soothes.** VCA says headbutting is also a way cats self-soothe and enjoy the scent of their own pheromones.

## Headbutt vs. other face rubbing

When marking objects like a couch or cat tree, cats use their cheek glands (VCA). A forehead-to-forehead bump with you is the social version.

## When a change matters

VCA says a cat that **used to headbutt but has lost interest** may not be feeling well, and if the change comes with other behavior changes, book a vet appointment to rule out illness.

## Supporting the scent bond

Cats rely on familiar scent to feel at home.
