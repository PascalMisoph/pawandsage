**Quick answer:** Yes. Chocolate contains **methylxanthines**, specifically **theobromine and caffeine**, according to Pet Poison Helpline. The ASPCA says methylxanthines can cause **vomiting and diarrhea, panting, excessive thirst and urination, hyperactivity, abnormal heart rhythm, tremors, seizures and even death**.

## Darker means more dangerous

The ASPCA says the **darker the chocolate** (the higher the cacao percentage) or the higher the caffeine content, the **greater the risk**. White chocolate has the lowest methylxanthine content, while baking chocolate and cocoa powder have the highest. Pet Poison Helpline adds that severity depends on the amount and type of chocolate and the size and health of the animal.

The ASPCA groups **coffee and caffeine** with chocolate for the same reason: methylxanthines.

## Watch the other ingredients

Pet Poison Helpline warns that chocolate candy and baked goods may also contain other poisons, including **raisins and xylitol**. Tell the vet everything that was in the treat.

## Symptoms to watch for

Pet Poison Helpline groups the signs as:

- **Gastrointestinal:** vomiting, diarrhea
- **Cardiovascular:** elevated heart rate, high blood pressure
- **Neurological:** hyperactivity, tremor, seizure

## What to do if your cat ate it

1. **Call right away:** your veterinarian, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply), or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies; the helpline says it is available 24/7).
2. **Have the details ready:** the product or food, how much and when, and your cat's weight and health conditions. Keep the packaging.
3. **Don't try home remedies** or make your cat vomit unless a veterinarian tells you to.
4. **Watch your cat** while you wait for advice, and go to an emergency clinic if you are told to.
