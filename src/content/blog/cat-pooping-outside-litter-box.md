**Quick answer:** Treat it like any house-soiling problem: vet check first, then the box. Cornell Feline Health Center lists **digestive problems** among the medical issues behind litter box trouble, and **arthritis** can make it hard to get into the box in time. After that, look at box size, cleanliness, litter and location.

## Medical causes to rule out

Cornell explains that any medical condition that interferes with normal defecation can cause litter box problems, and that digestive tract problems and conditions affecting mobility or cognitive function are possible culprits. Its cognitive dysfunction page notes arthritis may stop a cat getting into the box in time, and that cats with cognitive decline (usually 10 years or older) may eliminate outside the box.

Call your vet if you see diarrhea, constipation, straining, blood, or a cat that seems painful getting in and out.

## Box problems that hit poop first

Some cats pee in the box but poop elsewhere. Common reasons from the cited guidance:

- **The box is too small.** The AAFP/ISFM-based owner guide says boxes should be **1.5 times the cat's length** (nose to base of tail) and many commercial boxes are too small.
- **It isn't clean enough.** Some cats refuse a box with any waste in it (Cornell). Scoop at least daily (ASPCA). A second box gives your cat a clean option; see [how many litter boxes per cat](/blog/how-many-litter-boxes-per-cat).
- **High sides for an older cat.** Kittens and older cats need low sides (Cornell). The ASPCA notes older cats or cats with physical limitations may struggle with top-entry or high-sided boxes.
- **Litter they dislike.** Most cats prefer unscented litter (Cornell); many dislike scented or dusty litter (AAFP/ISFM-based guide). Change brands gradually; see [how to transition a cat to a new litter](/blog/how-to-transition-cat-to-new-litter).

## Location and stress

Cats prefer quiet, private spots where they cannot be cornered (AAFP/ISFM-based guide). Keep food and water away from the box. If another pet blocks or ambushes your cat, add a box in a different area. See [where to put a litter box](/blog/where-to-put-litter-box).

## Clean-up

Use an enzymatic cleaner (ASPCA), and avoid ammonia or vinegar products (Cornell). The ASPCA suggests making soiled spots less attractive, for example with aluminum foil or an upside-down carpet runner, while you fix the box.
