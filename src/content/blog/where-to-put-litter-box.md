**Quick answer:** Put the box somewhere **quiet, private and easy to reach**, **away from food and water**, and where your cat **can't be cornered**. Avoid loud appliances, busy hallways and spots next to the dog's bowls.

## Good spots

- **A quiet room your cat can always get into** (guest bath, spare room, quiet corner of a bedroom). VCA says a quiet but accessible location.
- **One on each floor** of a multi-level home (VCA; AAFP/ISFM-based owner guide).
- **Somewhere with a view and two ways out.** Cornell advises positioning the box so it isn't surrounded on three sides.

## Spots to avoid

- **Next to food and water:** the AAFP/ISFM-based guide says to keep them apart, and VCA says not directly next to the kitten's beds, water or food.
- **Next to the washer, dryer or furnace:** the ASPCA lists the washer and dryer among busy, loud or intimidating places, and the AAFP/ISFM-based guide mentions cats startled by loud appliances.
- **High-traffic areas** and anywhere a cat can be trapped, like a closet another cat can block (AAFP/ISFM-based guide; ASPCA).
- **Behind closed doors** your cat can't open.

## Small apartments

You still need a quiet, accessible spot. A bathroom corner or a piece of [litter box furniture](/blog/best-litter-box-furniture) in the living room can work if your cat accepts it. Leave the lid off if your cat hesitates; Cornell notes covers hold odors in. Our [Tuft & Paw Cove vs Cubby](/blog/tuft-and-paw-cove-vs-cubby) comparison covers two designer options.

## How to move a box without accidents

Cornell's method: move it **a few inches a day** toward the new spot. If your cat stops using it, move it back to the last spot it used reliably and start again more slowly. If your cat already prefers a new spot, try putting a box there first.
