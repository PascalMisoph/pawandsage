**Quick answer:** Yes, the plant. The ASPCA lists basil (*Ocimum basilicum*, including sweet basil and Thai basil) as **non-toxic to cats and dogs**. Concentrated **basil essential oil** is different: Texas A&M's veterinary college includes basil on its list of essential oils that are toxic to pets.

## What the ASPCA plant database says

- **Scientific name:** *Ocimum basilicum*
- **Family:** Lamiaceae
- **Other common names:** sweet basil, Thai basil, Saint Joseph's wort
- **ASPCA verdict:** Non-toxic to cats

The verdict above comes straight from the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center's entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/basil). ASPCA Poison Control is the reference most US veterinarians use for plant questions.

## Kitchen herbs: what else is on the windowsill?

Basil often shares a windowsill with other herbs that are not equally safe. On the ASPCA list, [rosemary](/blog/is-rosemary-safe-for-cats) is also non-toxic, but **mint** is listed as toxic in large amounts ([is peppermint safe for cats?](/blog/is-peppermint-oil-safe-for-cats)), and chives belong to the onion family, which Pet Poison Helpline lists as poisonous to cats ([onions and garlic](/blog/can-cats-eat-onions-and-garlic)).

## Basil in cooked food

The plant verdict does not cover a plate of pesto or a seasoned sauce. Those often contain garlic, onion and salt, which are a problem for cats. Keep human dishes away from your cat.

## Essential oil

The Merck Veterinary Manual says cats are more sensitive to essential oils than dogs because they lack enough of a liver enzyme (glucuronyl transferase). Do not apply basil oil to a cat, and do not run a diffuser in a room your cat cannot leave.

## When to call the vet anyway

Non-toxic means the plant is not expected to poison your cat, not that it is food. Call your veterinarian if your cat vomits repeatedly, seems lethargic, stops eating, or if you are not sure the plant really was basil (look-alikes and nicknames cause a lot of confusion). If you think your cat ate something toxic, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply).
