**Quick answer:** Switch **gradually**. Cornell Feline Health Center says to add small amounts of the new litter to the old. Alternatively, the ASPCA suggests offering a **side-by-side choice** of litters so your cat can pick. Either way, keep the old box available until your cat is reliably using the new litter.

## Method 1: Gradual mix

1. Start with your current litter, then mix in a small portion of the new one.
2. Each time you do a full change, increase the share of new litter.
3. Stop and step back if your cat hesitates, perches on the edge, stops covering, or goes beside the box. Cornell lists those as signs of litter box aversion.

## Method 2: Side-by-side choice

Set up a second box with the new litter next to the original. The ASPCA recommends this kind of choice test, and VCA suggests a "buffet" of box and litter styles for kittens. Once your cat picks the new box consistently, you can retire the old one. (Long term, the AAFP/ISFM-based guide suggests keeping boxes apart, because cats may treat boxes next to each other as one big box.)

## Picking a litter your cat is likely to accept

- **Unscented:** most cats prefer it (Cornell).
- **Soft, clumping texture:** most cats prefer soft unscented clumping litter, and many dislike aromatic or dusty litters (AAFP/ISFM-based guide). VCA says most cats prefer clumping-style litter.

## Switching to an automatic box at the same time?

Our suggestion: don't change box and litter in the same week. Change one thing, wait until your cat is comfortable, then change the next. For robot boxes, use a compatible litter; see [best litter for automatic litter boxes](/blog/best-litter-for-litter-robot).
