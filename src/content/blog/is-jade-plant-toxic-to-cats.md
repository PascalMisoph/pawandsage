**Quick answer:** Yes. The ASPCA lists the jade plant (*Crassula argentea*) as **toxic to cats, dogs and horses**. The toxic principle is listed as **unknown**, and the signs are **vomiting, depression and incoordination**.

## What the ASPCA plant database says

- **Scientific name:** *Crassula argentea*
- **Family:** Crassulaceae
- **Other common names:** baby jade, dwarf rubber plant, jade tree, Chinese rubber plant, Japanese rubber plant
- **ASPCA verdict:** Toxic to cats
- **Toxic principle:** unknown
- **Clinical signs listed:** vomiting, depression, incoordination

The verdict above comes straight from the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center's entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/jade-plant). ASPCA Poison Control is the reference most US veterinarians use for plant questions.

## Watch for wobbliness, not just vomiting

Most plant poisonings show up in the stomach. Jade is worth singling out because the ASPCA also lists **depression and incoordination**: a cat that seems flat, unsteady or "drunk" after chewing a succulent needs a call to your vet or poison control right away.

## "Rubber plant" can mean different things

The ASPCA lists "dwarf rubber plant," "Chinese rubber plant" and "Japanese rubber plant" as other names for jade. That is confusing, because the ASPCA also lists the Indian rubber plant (a ficus) as toxic, while the "American rubber plant" and "baby rubber plant" (*Peperomia*) are listed as non-toxic. Use the scientific name when you check a plant.

## Are succulents in general safe?

There is no single answer: succulents are many different plants. On the ASPCA list, jade and [aloe](/blog/is-aloe-vera-toxic-to-cats) are toxic. Check each plant individually in the [cat safety hub](/guides/is-it-safe-for-cats).

## Symptoms to watch for

According to the ASPCA, signs after a cat chews jade plant can include:

- Vomiting
- Depression
- Incoordination

How sick a cat gets depends on how much it ate, its size and its overall health, which is why poison control asks for those details.

## What to do if your cat ate jade plant

1. **Move your cat away from the plant** and remove any plant pieces you can easily reach without being bitten.
2. **Call right away:** your veterinarian, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply), or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies; the helpline says it is available 24/7).
3. **Have the details ready:** the plant's name (photo or label), roughly how much was eaten and when, and your cat's weight and health conditions.
4. **Don't try home remedies** or make your cat vomit unless a veterinarian tells you to.
5. **Bring the plant or a photo** if you are told to go to a clinic.
