**Quick answer:** Yes, in small amounts, if they are **fully cooked and plain**. VCA Animal Hospitals lists **cooked eggs** among foods that are safe and often enjoyed by cats, offered **without seasonings**. **Raw** or undercooked eggs are a different story.

## Why cooked, not raw

The ASPCA warns that raw eggs can carry bacteria such as **Salmonella and E. coli**, harmful to pets and people, and that raw eggs contain an enzyme that can **interfere with the absorption of certain vitamins**, leading to skin and coat problems. Cooking the egg through avoids both issues.

## What to leave out of the pan

The egg is not the problem; the extras are:

- **Onion, garlic or chives:** the ASPCA says Allium species can damage red blood cells and that cats are more susceptible than dogs. See [can cats eat onions and garlic?](/blog/can-cats-eat-onions-and-garlic)
- **Milk, cream or cheese:** the ASPCA says pets lack significant amounts of lactase, so dairy can cause diarrhea or digestive upset.
- **Salt:** the ASPCA lists excessively salty foods among foods to avoid.
- **Butter and oil:** keep it plain; VCA recommends low-calorie treats, and fatty extras add calories fast.

Cook a small amount of plain egg in a non-stick pan with nothing added, let it cool, and offer a bite-sized piece.

## How much is "a little"?

Human food counts toward your cat's treat budget. VCA Animal Hospitals says treats and snacks should make up no more than **10% of daily calories**, Cornell Feline Health Center gives a rule of thumb of **10 to 15%**, and WSAVA says **under 10%**. WSAVA's own table puts that at roughly **18 to 28 calories a day** for a healthy adult cat weighing 2.5 to 7 kg (about 5.5 to 15 lb). Your veterinarian can tell you what your cat needs.

## When to call the vet

Call your veterinarian if your cat vomits repeatedly, has diarrhea that does not settle, seems lethargic or stops eating, or if the food contained anything toxic such as onion, garlic, chocolate, grapes or xylitol. For a suspected poisoning, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply) or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies).
