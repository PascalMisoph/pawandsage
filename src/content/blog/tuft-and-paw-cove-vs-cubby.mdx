## Where Cove Earns Its Higher Price

Cove's main idea is that the cleaning kit lives on the box. Tuft & Paw designed the scoop to match the box's corners and gives the scoop, dustpan and brush their own parking spots. If your current setup is a scoop on the floor and a dustpan in another room, that matters every day.

The tub is also more substantial: double-walled, 7.3 lb before tools, with a textured recessed base so you can lift it, a hidden handle and an anti-slip rubber base. If you move the box to clean underneath or have a digger who shoves boxes around, Cove is the steadier choice.

## Where Cubby Is the Smarter Buy

Cubby keeps the part that solves most mess problems, the 8-inch removable walls, and drops the rest. It is lighter, a little smaller, and costs far less. Tuft & Paw also says it fits inside its Haven and Rifiuti litter box furniture, which makes it the one to pick if you want to hide the box in a cabinet.

You will need a scoop. Tuft & Paw sells a [Litter Box Scoop](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=59149&awinaffid=3141803&ued=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.tuftandpaw.com%2Fproducts%2Flitter-box-scoop) with wide slats that works with tofu pellets; any sturdy scoop works with clay.

## What Both Boxes Get Right for Cats

Both are open-top. Tuft & Paw's reasoning is that covered boxes can feel cramped, and the semi-transparent walls let a cat watch the room while still blocking kicked litter and high urine. If your cat has never used a high-walled box, Tuft & Paw suggests putting the new box where the old one was, using the same litter, and removing the walls at first if your cat seems unsure.

If your cat is suddenly going outside the box, see a vet before you buy anything. Cornell's Feline Health Center lists urinary inflammation, kidney disease, thyroid disease and diabetes among medical causes of litter box problems.
