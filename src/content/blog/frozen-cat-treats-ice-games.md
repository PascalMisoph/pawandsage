Frozen treats are the rare summer hack that does two jobs at once: they cool your cat from the inside and give them something genuinely interesting to work at. On a hot afternoon when a wand toy is too much, a frozen lick or an ice game keeps a cat engaged while gently bringing their temperature down — and most of these cost nothing but a few minutes and a freezer. A quick word on safety first, then the ideas.

## The Safety Rules (Read These First)

Frozen treats are only as good as what goes in them:

- **Keep portions small.** A little, often. You are offering a treat and a cool-down, not a meal, and a cat gulping something very cold too fast can get an upset stomach.
- **Cat-safe ingredients only.** Plain water, a spoon of your cat's wet food, or unsalted broth with **no onion, garlic, or seasoning**. Skip dairy — most cats are lactose intolerant — and never use sweeteners.
- **Supervise the first few times**, especially with ice cubes, and let cold treats sit a moment so they are not freezer-hard.

With that covered, here are the easy wins.

## 1. Frozen Wet-Food "Pucks"

Spoon your cat's usual wet food into an ice cube tray or silicone mold and freeze. Pop one out onto a saucer and let it soften for a minute. Your cat gets a long-lasting, cooling lick of food they already love, and it stretches a small amount into real engagement.

## 2. Broth Ice Cubes

Freeze plain, unsalted, onion- and garlic-free broth into cubes. Drop one in the water bowl to flavor and cool the water — a great trick for reluctant drinkers — or offer one on a plate as a lickable treat. It is hydration and enrichment in one. For more on keeping a cat drinking, see [how to get your cat to drink more water](/blog/get-cat-to-drink-more-water).

## 3. The Frozen Lick Mat

Spread a thin layer of wet food or a watery topper across a [LickiMat](https://amazon.com/dp/B089DN2HY7?tag=pawandsage21-20) and freeze it flat. The frozen texture makes your cat work much longer to lick it free — turning a thirty-second snack into a satisfying, self-soothing, cooling session. It is the single best frozen-enrichment tool because it lasts.

## 4. Ice Cube Hockey

The simplest game there is. Slide a plain ice cube across a hard floor and let your cat chase, bat, and "hunt" it. The cube moves unpredictably as it melts, which keeps it interesting, and a few licks along the way cool them down. Supervise so your cat bats rather than chews, and stick to a smooth floor.

## 5. The Floating Treat Bowl

Half-fill a wide bowl or shallow tray with water and float a favorite kibble or a piece of cat-safe meat on top, or freeze a treat into a large ice block so it is revealed slowly. Your cat fishes, paws, and works the water — a cooling, low-energy puzzle that taps right into their hunting instinct. It pairs perfectly with the ideas in [water play for cats](/blog/cat-water-play-cooling-games).

## 6. Frozen Toy Surprise

Freeze a small cat-safe toy or a few treats inside a cup of water to make an "ice block," then set it in a shallow tray. As it melts, your cat gets a slow-reveal puzzle and a cool surface to bat at. It is mess-contained enrichment that can occupy a curious cat for ages.

## Work These Into the Hot Hours

Frozen treats shine in exactly the window when active play is a bad idea — the hot middle of the day. Offer one as the afternoon enrichment, keep wand play for the cool morning and evening, and you have a cat that is engaged, cooler, and a little better hydrated all at once.
