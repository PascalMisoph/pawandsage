**Quick answer:** Yes. The ASPCA lists tulips (*Tulipa* species) as **toxic to cats, dogs and horses**. The toxic principles are **tulipalin A and B**, and the ASPCA notes the **highest concentration is in the bulb**.

## What the ASPCA plant database says

- **Scientific name:** *Tulipa spp.*
- **Family:** Liliaceae
- **ASPCA verdict:** Toxic to cats
- **Toxic principle:** tulipalin A and B
- **Clinical signs listed:** vomiting, depression, diarrhea, hypersalivation (drooling)

The verdict above comes straight from the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center's entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/tulip). ASPCA Poison Control is the reference most US veterinarians use for plant questions.

## Bouquets vs. bulbs

Cut tulips in a vase are the most common exposure for indoor cats: a nibbled petal or leaf, or water from the vase. Bulbs are the bigger worry where you store them for planting or force them indoors, because that is where the ASPCA says the toxin is most concentrated. Keep bags of bulbs sealed and out of reach.

## Mixed bouquets: check for lilies

Spring bouquets often mix tulips with true lilies. The U.S. FDA warns that true lilies and daylilies can cause fatal kidney failure in cats, which is a far more serious emergency. If your cat chewed a mixed bouquet, tell the vet or poison control everything that was in it.

## Safer flowers

Roses are listed as non-toxic by the ASPCA ([are roses toxic to cats?](/blog/are-roses-toxic-to-cats)), and moth orchids are a long-lasting non-toxic alternative ([are orchids toxic to cats?](/blog/are-orchids-toxic-to-cats)).

## Symptoms to watch for

According to the ASPCA, signs after a cat chews tulips can include:

- Vomiting
- Depression
- Diarrhea
- Hypersalivation (drooling)

How sick a cat gets depends on how much it ate, its size and its overall health, which is why poison control asks for those details.

## What to do if your cat ate tulips

1. **Move your cat away from the plant** and remove any plant pieces you can easily reach without being bitten.
2. **Call right away:** your veterinarian, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply), or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies; the helpline says it is available 24/7).
3. **Have the details ready:** the plant's name (photo or label), roughly how much was eaten and when, and your cat's weight and health conditions.
4. **Don't try home remedies** or make your cat vomit unless a veterinarian tells you to.
5. **Bring the plant or a photo** if you are told to go to a clinic.
