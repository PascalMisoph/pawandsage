There is a version of cat enrichment advice that does not work in July: wear your cat out with twenty minutes of flat-out wand play. On a hot day, that is not just unappealing to your cat — it is a route to overheating. But indoor cats actually get *more* bored in summer, not less, because heat makes them sleep more and move less until restlessness sets in. The answer is to switch the kind of enrichment you offer: low-energy, brain-first games that satisfy a cat without raising their temperature. Here are the best ones.

## Lead With the Brain, Not the Body

Physical exercise only tires half of a cat; the other half is mental, and mental work is exactly what suits a hot day because a cat can do it lying still in a cool spot. Foraging, problem-solving, and scent work leave a cat genuinely satisfied without a single sprint. If you want the full version of this idea, our guide to [mental stimulation for cats](/blog/mental-stimulation-for-cats) goes deep, but here is the summer-specific shortlist.

## 1. Turn a Meal Into a Foraging Puzzle

The highest-impact swap there is: stop serving food in a bowl and make your cat work for it. A puzzle feeder stretches a ninety-second meal into ten or fifteen minutes of nudging and thinking, all done in place.

**What helps:** A maze-style [Cat Amazing puzzle feeder](https://amazon.com/dp/B009R3SFBC?tag=pawandsage21-20) gives a cat a real problem to reason through, ideal for an afternoon when they would rather not move much.

## 2. Set Up Scent Work With a Snuffle Mat

Cats experience the world through their nose, and sniffing out food is mentally tiring in a quiet, low-effort way — perfect for heat.

**What helps:** A [snuffle mat](https://amazon.com/dp/B0CBV7H8XT?tag=pawandsage21-20) hides kibble in folds of fabric your cat works free with their nose. It is the gentlest form of "hunting" there is, and it keeps a cat absorbed without raising a sweat.

## 3. Slow Things Down With a Lick Mat

A lick mat spread with a thin layer of wet food turns a snack into a few minutes of focused, self-soothing licking. Use a [LickiMat](https://amazon.com/dp/B089DN2HY7?tag=pawandsage21-20), and on a hot day you can pop it in the freezer first for a cooling version — gentle enrichment and a chilled treat in one. For more on that, see [DIY frozen cat treats and ice games](/blog/frozen-cat-treats-ice-games).

## 4. Scatter-Feed Around a Cool Room

Take part of a meal and tuck small portions in easy "find spots" around a shaded room — behind a table leg, on a low shelf, in a box. Your cat gets a reason to patrol and search instead of stewing in boredom, and it costs nothing.

## 5. Run "Cat TV" From a Shaded Perch

A window view is all-day passive enrichment that asks nothing of your cat physically. The summer trick is placement: set the perch back from hot glass, in the shaded part of the room, so they get the entertainment without the radiant heat. A bird feeder on the glass turns an empty window into a live channel.

## 6. Rotate Toys Instead of Adding Them

Novelty is mental stimulation. Keep three or four toys in play and box the rest, then swap weekly — the same toy comes back genuinely new to your cat. It is free, and it keeps interest up through a sluggish season. If your cat ignores toys entirely, the cause is usually fixable: see [why your cat won't play with toys](/blog/cat-wont-play-with-toys).

## Keep the Active Play — Just Time It Right

None of this means dropping physical play. Cats still need it; you just move it to the cool edges of the day. Do your wand sessions in the early morning and the evening, let your cat actually catch the toy at the end, and keep the hot midday hours for the brain games above.

A well-enriched summer cat usually has one foraging meal a day, a scent or lick task for the hot hours, a shaded window channel running, and a short active session when it cools. That mix beats boredom without ever putting your cat at risk of overheating. For the wider picture, see [how to keep your cat cool in hot weather](/blog/keep-cat-cool-hot-weather).
