**Quick answer:** Yes. The ASPCA lists the snake plant (*Sansevieria trifasciata*, also called mother-in-law's tongue) as **toxic to cats and dogs**. Its toxic principle is **saponins**, and the listed signs are **nausea, vomiting and diarrhea**.

## What the ASPCA plant database says

- **Scientific name:** *Sansevieria trifasciata*
- **Family:** Agavaceae
- **Other common names:** mother-in-law's tongue, golden bird's nest, good luck plant
- **ASPCA verdict:** Toxic to cats
- **Toxic principle:** saponins
- **Clinical signs listed:** nausea, vomiting, diarrhea

The verdict above comes straight from the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center's entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/snake-plant). ASPCA Poison Control is the reference most US veterinarians use for plant questions.

## How serious is it?

Compared with plants that damage the kidneys or heart, the ASPCA's listed signs for snake plant are digestive: nausea, vomiting and diarrhea. That still means a sick cat, and the amount eaten, your cat's size and health all matter, so call your vet or poison control rather than guessing.

## Why cats leave it alone (mostly)

Snake plants have stiff, upright leaves, so many cats ignore them. Kittens and bored cats are the exception. If yours chews leaf tips, move the plant out of reach and add more play; [signs your cat is bored](/blog/signs-cat-is-bored) covers what else to look for.

## Similar-looking plants

Snake plant is now often sold under the name *Dracaena trifasciata*, and the ASPCA also lists [dracaena (including lucky bamboo)](/blog/is-lucky-bamboo-toxic-to-cats) as toxic to cats. Either label means: keep it away from cats.

## Symptoms to watch for

According to the ASPCA, signs after a cat chews snake plant can include:

- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea

How sick a cat gets depends on how much it ate, its size and its overall health, which is why poison control asks for those details.

## What to do if your cat ate snake plant

1. **Move your cat away from the plant** and remove any plant pieces you can easily reach without being bitten.
2. **Call right away:** your veterinarian, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply), or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies; the helpline says it is available 24/7).
3. **Have the details ready:** the plant's name (photo or label), roughly how much was eaten and when, and your cat's weight and health conditions.
4. **Don't try home remedies** or make your cat vomit unless a veterinarian tells you to.
5. **Bring the plant or a photo** if you are told to go to a clinic.
