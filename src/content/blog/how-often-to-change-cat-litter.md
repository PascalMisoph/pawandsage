**Quick answer:** **Scoop at least once a day.** For a full litter change, the ASPCA says to **wash the box and completely replace the litter once a week**. The owner guide based on the AAFP and ISFM guidelines says to **wash the box every 1–4 weeks** with soap and hot water.

## The schedule, side by side

| Task | ASPCA | AAFP/ISFM-based owner guide |
|---|---|---|
| Remove waste | At least once a day | At least once a day, top up litter as needed |
| Wash the box | Once a week, warm water and unscented soap, baking soda, or no soap | Every 1–4 weeks, soap and hot water only |
| Replace all litter | Once a week, with the wash | (Not specified separately) |
| Avoid | Scented cleaners (cats may develop an aversion) | Strong chemicals and ammonia-based products |

## Why daily scooping matters

- **Your cat cares.** Cornell Feline Health Center says some cats refuse a box with any waste in it, and an unclean box is a top cause of avoidance.
- **Your household's health.** The CDC advises changing the litter box **daily** because the toxoplasmosis parasite does not become infectious until **1 to 5 days** after it is shed in feces. If you are pregnant or have a weakened immune system, ask your doctor about litter box precautions.
- **You spot problems early.** Scooping daily is how you notice more or bigger urine clumps, diarrhea, or no urine at all, all reasons to call your vet.

## Automatic litter boxes

Self-cleaning boxes rake waste for you, but they still need emptying and periodic deep cleaning. The ASPCA notes many cats accept them readily, but if your cat starts eliminating outside the box, switch back to a traditional box.
