**Quick answer:** No. Pet Poison Helpline says onion, garlic, chives and leeks (the **Allium** family) are **poisonous to cats**, and that **cats are more sensitive** than many other animals. The ASPCA says Alliums can cause **gastrointestinal irritation and red blood cell damage that can lead to anemia**.

## How Alliums harm cats

Pet Poison Helpline explains that poisonous doses of Alliums **damage red blood cells, leading to anemia**, and cause GI distress with nausea, vomiting and diarrhea. The ASPCA notes that although **cats are more susceptible**, dogs are also at risk depending on the amount ingested.

## Where Alliums hide

It is rarely a raw onion. More often it is something cooked with onion or garlic:

- Gravies, sauces, soups and stocks
- Seasoned meats, sausages, takeout and leftovers
- Garlic bread, herbed cheeses and dips
- Holiday dishes such as stuffing ([can cats eat turkey?](/blog/can-cats-eat-turkey-thanksgiving))

The family also includes shallots and green onions (Pet Poison Helpline) and chives, which the ASPCA lists as toxic to cats on its plant list. VCA and WSAVA both list onions and garlic among foods cats should avoid.

## What about garlic as a "natural" flea remedy?

Don't. Garlic is an Allium and is poisonous to cats according to Pet Poison Helpline. Ask your veterinarian for a flea product made and labeled for cats.

## What to do if your cat ate it

1. **Call right away:** your veterinarian, the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply), or Pet Poison Helpline at [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661) (an incident fee applies; the helpline says it is available 24/7).
2. **Have the details ready:** the product or food, how much and when, and your cat's weight and health conditions. Keep the packaging.
3. **Don't try home remedies** or make your cat vomit unless a veterinarian tells you to.
4. **Watch your cat** while you wait for advice, and go to an emergency clinic if you are told to.
