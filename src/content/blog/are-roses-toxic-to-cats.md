**Quick answer:** No. The ASPCA lists roses (*Rosa* species) as **non-toxic to cats, dogs and horses**. A cat nibbling rose petals is not a poisoning emergency.

## What the ASPCA plant database says

- **Scientific name:** *Rosa species*
- **Family:** Rosaceae
- **ASPCA verdict:** Non-toxic to cats

The verdict above comes straight from the [ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center's entry](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants/rose). ASPCA Poison Control is the reference most US veterinarians use for plant questions.

## The bouquet is the real risk

Roses usually arrive with company. Mixed bouquets can include true lilies, which the U.S. FDA warns can cause **fatal kidney failure in cats**, plus other flowers on the toxic list. Before a bouquet goes on the table, check every stem, and throw out lilies entirely if you have a cat. Our [holiday plants chart](/blog/holiday-plants-toxic-to-cats) lists the common bouquet offenders.

Also keep the little packet of cut-flower food and the vase water away from cats; the plant verdict covers the rose, not additives.

## Not every "rose" is a rose

Plants like "rose of Sharon" and "rosebay" share the name but are different plants. The ASPCA lists them separately; rosebay appears on its toxic list. Check the scientific name (*Rosa*) if you are unsure.

## Thorns

Thorny stems are a physical hazard rather than a toxic one. Strip thorns from stems in a vase your cat can reach, or keep roses out of jumping range.

## When to call the vet anyway

Non-toxic means the plant is not expected to poison your cat, not that it is food. Call your veterinarian if your cat vomits repeatedly, seems lethargic, stops eating, or if you are not sure the plant really was roses (look-alikes and nicknames cause a lot of confusion). If you think your cat ate something toxic, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435) (a consultation fee may apply).
