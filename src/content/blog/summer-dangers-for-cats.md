Summer is wonderful for cats, but the season quietly stacks the house and garden with hazards they do not face the rest of the year. Fresh bouquets, open windows, BBQ leftovers, garden chemicals — all arrive together in June and July. None of it is cause for panic; almost every summer danger is easy to manage once you know it exists. Here is a room-by-room run-through so nothing catches you off guard.

## In the Vase: Lilies and Cut Flowers

This is the most important one, so it comes first. **Lilies are deadly to cats.** True lilies and daylilies are so toxic that a cat brushing past the pollen and grooming it off, nibbling a single leaf, or even drinking the vase water can suffer fatal kidney failure. Summer bouquets are full of them.

If you have a cat, the safest rule is simply no lilies in the house, ever. If a lily does come in and your cat may have contacted it, treat it as an emergency and call your vet immediately — early treatment is the difference between a scare and a tragedy. Other common cut-flower and houseplant risks include hydrangea, oleander, and sago palm.

## In the Garden: Plants and Chemicals

If your cat has any outdoor access, the garden adds its own list:

- **Toxic plants:** foxglove, oleander, azalea, many bulbs, and more. Check the [ASPCA's toxic plant database](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants) before planting.
- **Slug and snail pellets** containing metaldehyde are highly poisonous — use pet-safe alternatives.
- **Weedkillers, fertilizers, and insecticides** should be kept off areas your cat walks, and stored sealed.
- **Cocoa mulch** can contain the same toxin as chocolate.

The simplest fix is to grow a cat-safe space instead — our guide to building a [cat-safe sensory garden](/blog/cat-sensory-garden-summer) covers what you *can* plant to delight your cat rather than endanger them.

## At the BBQ: Food and Drink Hazards

Summer entertaining means tempting smells and dropped scraps. Keep cats away from:

- **Onions, garlic, and chives** — in many marinades, dips, and sauces; toxic to cats even cooked.
- **Cooked bones**, which splinter, and **fatty trimmings**, which can trigger pancreatitis.
- **Grapes, raisins, chocolate, and alcohol** — all dangerous.
- **Xylitol**, a sweetener in some drinks, gum, and desserts.
- **Corn cobs and skewers**, serious choking and blockage risks.

A cat-safe treat or a little plain cooked meat keeps them happily included without the danger.

## Open Windows, Hot Cars, and Other Physical Risks

Not every summer danger is something they eat:

- **Open windows and balconies** cause a seasonal spike in falls — see [cat window and balcony safety](/blog/cat-window-balcony-safety).
- **Hot cars and sealed rooms** can become lethal in minutes; never confine a cat in one.
- **Sheds, garages, and greenhouses** trap cats that wander in and bake or starve — check before closing them.
- **Garden tools, twine, and netting** can entangle a curious cat.

## Bites, Stings, and Parasites

Warm weather brings more bugs, and more interactions with them:

- **Bee and wasp stings** are usually minor, but a sting in the mouth or throat, or signs of an allergic reaction (swelling, difficulty breathing), is an emergency.
- **Fleas and ticks** peak now — stay on top of prevention, covered in [how to get rid of fleas on cats](/blog/get-rid-of-fleas-on-cats).
- **Overheating** is itself a top summer danger; know the [signs of heatstroke](/blog/heatstroke-in-cats).

## Keep These Two Numbers Handy

Before the season gets busy, save your **vet's number** and a **pet poison helpline** somewhere you can find them fast. With poisoning, minutes matter, and knowing exactly who to call removes the panic from a scary moment.

Summer hazards look like a long list, but managing them is mostly a handful of habits: no lilies, cat-safe plants, scraps off-limits, windows secured, and sheds checked. Do those, and the season goes back to being what it should be for your cat — the best one of the year.
